Six waveform models in JAX and MLX, checked against LALSimulation

How TaylorF2, IMRPhenomD, IMRPhenomXAS, IMRPhenomXHM, the precessing IMRPhenomXPNR and the surrogate NRSur7dq4, and LALSimulation's conversions between the time and frequency domains, were written as pure functions that compile, vectorise and differentiate, in JAX on the CPU and in MLX on Apple GPUs, in float64 and float32. The report covers how they compare with LALSimulation 6.2.0, with waveformrs and with each other, how their derivatives compare with finite differences, and what they cost.

Summary

waveformx implements six models in JAX: the frequency-domain models TaylorF2, IMRPhenomD, IMRPhenomXAS, IMRPhenomXHM and the precessing IMRPhenomXPNR, and the time-domain surrogate NRSur7dq4, with the interface of waveformrs's Python bindings and LALSimulation's conventions. Every model is a pure function of a Binary pytree, so it can be compiled with jax.jit, evaluated for many binaries at once with jax.vmap, and differentiated with respect to all twelve parameters of the binary with jax.grad, jax.jvp and jax.jacfwd. LALSimulation's SimInspiralTD and SimInspiralFD are reproduced as well, with LAL's lengths and start times, and are differentiable too. NRSur7dq4 follows LAL's code branch for branch, with each branch written as traced arithmetic, and agrees with LALSimulation 6.2.0 to rounding. IMRPhenomXPNR keeps LAL's physics but not its numerical procedure: it uses the SpinTaylor precession angles for every binary, solved once with a high-order integrator, with LAL's discrete switches made continuous and the parity fix of lalsuite !2593. Where LAL uses the SpinTaylor angles the two agree to small mismatches; the version that reproduced LAL's numerics step for step is commit 0bbd3c5, tag lal-faithful (section 3). Every model also runs in MLX on Apple GPUs, every model can take its reference frequency scaled by the total mass (reference_mf), and no model computes in SI units.

In short:

  • Implementation. 134 Python modules (34,120 lines, of which 3,911 are parameter-space fits generated from waveformrs's Rust by a translator) cover the six models, in JAX and MLX, their checks, the conversions and the matches (sections 2–8).
  • Accuracy. NRSur7dq4 agrees with LAL at rounding level. IMRPhenomXPNR agrees with LAL with the parity fix to a median mismatch of 2.8×10-8 where LAL keeps the SpinTaylor angles; it differs by more where LAL switches to other angles, which the model does not use, and by up to ~2×10−4 for some heavy binaries, where it attaches the merger-ringdown angles with exact derivatives rather than LAL's spline ends (section 14). The other models agree with LAL to rounding or close to it (section 10).
  • Single precision. Every model runs in float32 when the binary's parameters are float32, and returns the strain times 1021 by default. For NRSur7dq4 in float32, an inner product between two different waveforms changes by 2.4×10-6 of the product of their norms (median; at most 1.0×10-4). Derivatives agree with float64 to 5.6×10-5 (section 7).
  • Apple GPUs. NRSur7dq4 also runs in MLX on Apple GPUs, in float32, with its derivatives, LAL's grids and the conditioned waveforms. It is as accurate as JAX's float32 version: the overlap of two binaries changes by 2.3×10-6 (median). In batches it evaluates 41,208 waveforms per second, 27.7× JAX's best on this machine's CPU (section 7).
  • IMRPhenomXPNR on Apple GPUs. IMRPhenomXPNR runs in MLX, in float32, with its derivatives, generated from the JAX model's own jaxprs. Its mismatch with float64 is as small as JAX's float32 one (6.4×10-9 median). In batches it evaluates 3,626 waveforms per second, 26.0× JAX's best on this machine's CPU (section 3).
  • IMRPhenomXAS. The aligned-spin (2,2) model is public, written for speed in JAX and in MLX: each binary's coefficients form a table, and each frequency costs a short formula. It agrees with LAL to 2.4×10-10 in float64. In float32 the leading term of the inspiral phase is computed in extended precision, so binary neutron stars from 20 Hz keep mismatches of at most 7.4×10-8 in MLX and 3.4×10-8 in JAX. One binary on 220 frequencies takes 5.75 ms in JAX, 8.6× faster than LAL, and 566 µs in MLX (section 4).
  • IMRPhenomXHM. The aligned-spin model with higher modes is public in the same form, LAL's current release on one code path, with its modes as well as its polarizations. Its polarizations agree with LAL to mismatches below 5.1×10-8 in float64; in float32 binary neutron stars from 20 Hz keep mismatches of at most 7.3×10-8 in MLX. In MLX batches of 4096 binaries on 1000 frequencies reach 543,075 waveforms per second (section 5).
  • TaylorF2 and IMRPhenomD. Both are public in the same form as IMRPhenomXAS, in JAX and in MLX. TaylorF2 agrees with LAL to 3.8×10-10 in float64. IMRPhenomD replaces LAL's interpolated table of quasi-normal-mode frequencies with closed-form fits in the final spin, no less accurate than LAL's spline, and agrees with LAL to mismatches below 6.6×10-10. In MLX, batches reach 5,915,656 (TaylorF2) and 4,915,199 (IMRPhenomD) waveforms of 1000 frequencies per second (section 6).
  • Against NRSur7dq4. waveformx.match gives matches maximised over time, phase or polarisation as pure JAX functions, and optimises them over the conventions that differ between models. On a fixed set of 40 binaries at 90–200 M☉, our IMRPhenomXPNR departs from LAL 6.2.0's almost only through the parity fix. Where LAL's IMRPhenomXPNR and NRSur7dq4 disagree, ours is closer to NRSur7dq4 for 14 of 20 binaries (median mismatch ratio 0.89); where they agree, for 9 of 20 (section 11).
  • Smooth in the parameters. IMRPhenomXPNR is continuous where LAL switches discretely: through vanishing in-plane spins (it changes by 10−12 for an in-plane spin of 10−12), at equal masses and across LAL's thresholds in the angles. The steps that belong to LAL's model rather than its numerics, such as that of the (3,3) mode across zero total aligned spin, are kept (section 3).
  • A reference scaled by the mass. With reference_mf every model gives the binary at the frequency reference_mf/M, the same point of the inspiral for every mass; converters map binaries between this convention and LAL's. For IMRPhenomXPNR the waveform is then independent of the reference chosen (section 3).
  • Derivatives. IMRPhenomXPNR is differentiated in forward mode, and its reverse-mode gradient is the transpose of that forward-mode Jacobian. NRSur7dq4 is differentiated directly in either mode. NRSur7dq4's derivatives agree with finite differences to ~1e-8, IMRPhenomXPNR's to 1.9×10-7 at worst (section 12).
  • Cost. A compiled IMRPhenomXPNR waveform takes 7.81 ms, against 13.82 ms for LALSimulation. jax.vmap over many binaries is how JAX code would use it. Compilation takes 11.9 s for the model, 17.9 s under jax.vmap and 47.4 s for its gradient. Long waveforms and batches are evaluated in chunks and blocks, so the memory of a jax.vmap of Jacobians stays within a few GB (section 13).

Models as pure JAX functions

The package has the interface of waveformrs's Python bindings, with the same function names, keyword arguments, options, result classes and exceptions. Underneath, every model is a pure function of a Binary: a NamedTuple of float64 arrays (or float32 arrays), and so a JAX pytree.

import jax, jax.numpy as jnp, numpy as np
import waveformx as wf

frequencies = np.arange(20.0, 1024.0, 0.125)
hp, hc = wf.polarizations("IMRPhenomXPNR", frequencies, mass_1=36.0, mass_2=29.0,
                          spin_1=(0.4, 0.2, 0.3), spin_2=(-0.2, 0.3, -0.1), f_ref=20.0)

# the pure function, for jit, vmap and grad
model = wf.polarizations_function("IMRPhenomXPNR")          # f(binary, frequencies)
binary = wf.Binary(mass_1=36.0, mass_2=29.0, spin_1=jnp.array([0.4, 0.2, 0.3]),
                   spin_2=jnp.array([-0.2, 0.3, -0.1]), f_ref=20.0)
loss = lambda b: jnp.sum(jnp.abs(model(b, frequencies)[0]) ** 2)
jax.grad(loss)(binary)                                      # a Binary of derivatives
jax.vmap(model, in_axes=(0, None))(binaries, frequencies)    # many binaries at once

# NRSur7dq4 gives the strain times 1e21 (scaled_strain=False: the strain itself)
h = wf.td_polarizations("NRSur7dq4", 1 / 4096, 20.0, mass_1=40.0, mass_2=20.0)
surrogate = wf.td_polarizations_function("NRSur7dq4")       # f(binary, times)
single = wf.Binary(mass_1=jnp.float32(40.0), mass_2=jnp.float32(20.0), spin_1=jnp.zeros(3, jnp.float32),
                   spin_2=jnp.zeros(3, jnp.float32), f_ref=20.0)
hp, hc = surrogate(single, h.times)                         # in float32
h = wf.simulate_td("IMRPhenomXPNR", 1 / 4096, 20.0, mass_1=36.0, mass_2=29.0)

A call goes through four stages:

  1. Checks on the hostThe public functions convert the arguments and run LALSimulation's checks on concrete values (MODEL.validate), outside any JAX transformation. Invalid masses, spins or frequencies raise the exceptions of waveformrs: InvalidParameterError, ModelError and so on.
  2. A compiled function per configurationThe model method is compiled with jax.jit with the options fixed. WaveformOptions is a frozen dataclass, so it can be a static argument. JAX caches the compiled function per options and array shape, so a sampler that keeps its frequency grid compiles once.
  3. Traced arithmeticInside, nothing branches in Python on the binary. Each of LAL's ifs becomes a jnp.where between branches that are both evaluated, with safe arguments, so that the branch not taken cannot put NaN into the value or the gradient. Loops of fixed length are lax.scan; loops whose length depends on the binary are lax.while_loop.
  4. Failures as NaNFailures that can only be detected while computing make the output NaN. The batch functions report those binaries as ModelError in errors and set their rows to zero.

Importing the package enables 64-bit floats (jax_enable_x64), which IMRPhenomXPNR's comparisons with LAL need; both models also run in float32 (section 3, section 7). The thousands of fitted coefficients of IMRPhenomX are not copied by hand. scripts/rust_to_python.py parses the straight-line Rust of waveformrs (its let bindings, arithmetic, fma calls and f64 methods) and emits the same expressions on JAX arrays. scripts/generate_fits.py then writes them into models/xpnr/_generated/. The control flow is written by hand, file by file after LAL's, and each module's docstring names the LAL function it follows.

Each model package defines a MODEL with a fixed contract. A frequency-domain model has validate, polarizations, modes and choose_fd_modes. A time-domain model has validate, td_polarizations, td_modes and polarizations_at, the last of which evaluates the waveform at any given times. The public functions, the batches and the conversions are written once, against that contract.

IMRPhenomXPNR

IMRPhenomXPNR is IMRPhenomXPHM with the phenomenological PNR precession angles, a co-precessing (2,2) mode tuned to numerical relativity, an antisymmetric (2,|2|) contribution and the 122019 release of the higher modes. In LALSimulation it draws on many of the 49 IMRPhenomX source files, together with the SpinTaylor code and GSL.

LAL's physics, not its numerics

Up to commit 0bbd3c5 (tag lal-faithful) the package reproduced LALSimulation's numerical procedure step for step: GSL's adaptive RKF45 integration of the SpinTaylor equations replayed one step at a time, LAL's splines through its samples, the fallbacks to the NNLO and MSA angles, the released model without the parity fix, the roots of GSL's companion matrix, LAL's finite differences in the higher modes, single-precision storage and masses in kilograms. That version agrees with LAL 6.2.0 to the level at which different builds of LAL agree with each other, and its report describes how (below). Since then the model keeps LAL's physics, the fits, the ansatzes, the PNR construction of the angles and the calibrated choices of the higher-mode amplitudes, and computes it in the way best suited to JAX: the comparisons with LAL go through LAL itself or waveformrs, which reproduces LAL to rounding. What differs from LAL:

  • One prescription. LALSimulation chooses the Euler angles by the binary: in-plane spins below 1e-7 give plain IMRPhenomXPHM with the NNLO angles, below 1e-6 the PNR machinery runs with the NNLO angles (version 102), and where its SpinTaylor integration fails it falls back to the MSA angles (223), or to NNLO if the MSA system cannot be set up. The model uses the SpinTaylor angles (330) with the PNR machinery for every binary, so it compiles one prescription and is continuous through vanishing in-plane spins. Versions 102 and 223 are models of their own, IMRPhenomXPNR_NNLO and IMRPhenomXPNR_MSA, each used for every binary (below).
  • The SpinTaylor solution. The precession equations are solved once, in one Dormand–Prince 8(5,3) loop with dense output (spintaylor/lean.py): backward steps of equal increments of u = 1/(Mω), so that each step covers about the same precession angle, to below fmin, then forward steps from fref (at most a tenth of u each, which keeps the steps short near v = 1 for large aligned spins) until LAL's stopping conditions are met. A few hundred steps resolve a whole inspiral to ~1e-12, and the step size is a smooth function of the state, so the solution is differentiable. The angles are evaluated from the dense output, with no sampling lattice and no splines through millions of samples. The integration starts low enough for the grid of the PNR interpolants, so LAL's second integration from the start of that grid is not needed. The end of the inspiral is where the first stopping condition is met on the dense output, less half a step of LAL's fine sampling, found by bisection and refined by a Newton step that carries the derivatives. Where the stopping conditions already fail at the reference frequency (beyond the validity of the PN equations, where LAL gives up the SpinTaylor angles), the end is where they are first met on the backward solution.
  • Continuous switches. LAL's discrete switches in the angles are made continuous: the hand-off of the SpinTaylor α to its merger-ringdown continuation (at 0.97 of the end of the inspiral, where the two agree in value and slope, rather than 0.98), the two-spin construction (for every binary, rather than from |χ2| = 1e-3), the remapped J frame (for all in-plane spins, rather than from 1e-3), the refinement of the grid of the PNR interpolants near L ≈ J, the edge of the PNR transition region at q = 12 (blended over 12–14) and the choices of the connection frequencies of the PNR β (blended across bands of 0.002 in Mf). The PNR angles are interpolated with LAL's splines of α, β and γ: splines of the unit vector of L and of γ ± α would follow L passing close to J, but where LAL's grid lets α turn by up to ~3 rad between nodes, at its lower end, they cut across its circle (mismatches with LAL of up to 5×10−4), and refining the grid until they do not costs more than LAL's splines. They remain for the phase grid. LAL's splines are taken on a grid of its own rather than on LAL's uniform grid, whose spacing is what its lower end needs (0.01 Hz, its floor, for light binaries: up to ~300,000 nodes): the nodes span LAL's band with a spacing that grows like f2 (constant steps of the precession and nutation phases at leading order) from a quarter of LAL's spacing up to 10−4 in Mf, and the splines are taken in the index of the nodes. A few thousand nodes then follow LAL's splines to mismatches of 10−10 at most (median 10−16 over random binaries), closer than LAL's own grid is to its limit where that grid is coarse (3×10−8 for a 12.6 + 12.1 M☉ binary). The quarter of LAL's spacing at the start covers L passing close to J above the start, where LAL's grid is finer than it needs to be at the start; with LAL's spacing the f2 grid differs from LAL's by up to 3×10−6 for light binaries of q ≈ 10. The antisymmetric mode reads the PNR α below its transition from the same splines, plus the constant by which LAL's second evaluation of α there differs (the change of the offset of α), rather than evaluating the SpinTaylor α again at every frequency. A separate report (report/extended, measured at the tag) maps these switches and their sizes.
  • Vanishing spins. As the in-plane spins vanish, the waveform tends linearly to that of the aligned spins, and its derivative along each direction tends to the one-sided derivative there. Until this change it jumped by ~1e-3 between an in-plane spin of 1e-8 and none (at ι = 0.7; 2e-6 face-on), because LAL's construction leaves an opening angle where the in-plane spins vanish, about an α that is arbitrary there. LAL's arctan window (Eq. 62 of arXiv:2107.08876) keeps the PNR β at least 1.4e-3 rad from zero. The single-spin MSA system behind the taper of the two-spin oscillations perturbs its degenerate roots by 1e-9, which leaves β ≈ 3e-5. And the polar angle of the effective spin, kept 1e-15 from the poles, leaves an in-plane spin of ~5e-8 in the PNR fits. The model keeps β as it is within [0, π] (its splines of the unit vector of L are smooth through the poles) and goes over into LAL's window across 0.01 rad outside, where the window folds the merger-ringdown fits back. It evaluates the single-spin β as atan2(S⊥, L + S∥), LAL's MSA expression for a spin of constant magnitude without the perturbed roots, and gives the PNR fits of the form χ sin θ g(cos θ) and the PN waveform β the in-plane spin itself. The SpinTaylor β, the J frame, θJN and the polar angles of the spins are taken as atan2 of components rather than as acos of cosines, which lose half the digits of small angles and with them the derivatives. Now the waveform changes by 1e-12 for an in-plane spin of 1e-12, and as both spins vanish together it changes linearly too, except across zero total aligned spin, where the (3,3) mode switches (below). At zero in-plane spin itself the waveform has no derivative: LAL's fits of the co-precessing (2,2) mode and of the PNR angles depend on the magnitude of the in-plane spin, so the one-sided derivatives along opposite directions differ. There the derivatives are finite. At equal masses LAL's estimate of the period of the two-spin oscillations is NaN, and its grid of the PNR interpolants the coarse one. The limit q → 1 is the finest grid, which the model takes, removing a step of ~1e-3 at q = 1 (tests/test_xpnr_smooth.py).
  • Numerics. Masses stay in solar masses, the mixing coefficients of the (3,2) mode are not rounded to single precision, nothing keeps XLA from fusing multiply-adds, the connection frequencies of the PNR β are evaluated with finite stand-ins for LAL's NaNs (without replaying its NaN arithmetic), the higher modes take exact derivatives where LAL takes finite differences with steps of 1e-9 and 1e-7, and the quintic of the higher-mode amplitudes is tested for a zero crossing by its signs at 257 points instead of the roots of GSL's companion matrix.
  • The parity fix. The model includes the fix of lalsuite !2593 from LALSuite's development branch, as waveformrs does by default. It restores the mirror symmetry of the antisymmetric modes: flipping the in-plane spins and taking ι → π − ι gives (h+, −h×). The released LALSimulation 6.2.0 breaks it by more than 1e-4.

The physics switches that LAL makes on the binary in the higher modes are kept: the versions of the inspiral and intermediate amplitudes chosen by mass ratio and spin, the vetoes of collocation points, the lowering of the order of the intermediate polynomial where it would cross zero, the second intermediate region for extreme mass ratios (η thresholds), the inspiral ansatz in the ringdown of the (3,3) and (4,4) modes, the collocation points of the intermediate phase of the (3,3) mode, which change with the sign of the total aligned spin (a step of ~1e-3 in that mode across zero total spin), and the vanishing odd modes of equal masses with equal spins, which the odd modes approach continuously. They are LAL's model rather than its numerics; making them continuous would change the physics.

Against LAL with the parity fix the model agrees to mismatches of 1e-11 to 5e-5 where LAL uses the SpinTaylor angles (closest for two generic spins away from LAL's switches; up to ~2e-4 beyond q = 12, where the angles go smoothly to LAL's outside the transition region), and differs by up to a mismatch of ~0.3 where LAL falls back to the MSA or NNLO angles (section 10). Measured one at a time (report/xpnr_cost.py: 64 frequencies, all modes, tracing + lowering + compilation), it compiles in 11.9 s, under jax.vmap in 17.9 s and its gradient in 47.4 s, against 14.4 s, 19.6 s and 56.7 s for the version at the tag; at the benchmark binary it runs in 6.24 ms (10.9 ms at the tag).

The option extended_phase_grid interpolates the PNR angles with splines of the unit vector of L and of γ ± α on such a grid from LAL's spacing (rather than a quarter of it). For the benchmark binary it runs in 8.55 ms (against 6.24 ms); for a light binary (8 + 5 M☉ from 10 Hz on 16,225 frequencies) in 22.09 ms against 19.78 ms, at mismatches below 1e-4 from the default (tests/test_xpnr_smooth.py). It compiles in 12.2 s (47.9 s for the gradient).

The NNLO and MSA prescriptions

LAL's other two precession prescriptions are approximants of their own: IMRPhenomXPNR_NNLO builds the PNR angles on the NNLO angles (PhenomXPrecVersion 102) and IMRPhenomXPNR_MSA on the MSA angles (223). They are names rather than an option because the option LAL's dictionary offers, precession_version, already has waveformrs's meaning for IMRPhenomXPHM (default 320, and 223 in lalsimulation_defaults()); an approximant is static, with a compiled function of its own, and every public function, the conversions, the converters between reference conventions and the MLX port take it. The prescription is a static version of model.polarizations. Neither integrates the SpinTaylor equations: the set-up is finish_setup, populate_structs and the grid of the PNR interpolants of that version, and the rest of the model is shared. Each is used for every binary, without LAL's switches between versions (below in-plane spins of 1e-6 LAL's 223 takes the NNLO angles, and below 1e-7 both turn the PNR angles off); where the MSA system cannot be set up (LAL then takes the NNLO angles) the MSA model is NaN and the public functions raise ModelError. The converters between reference conventions are IMRPhenomXPNR's, which evolve the spins with the SpinTaylor equations.

Against LAL 6.2.0 with the parity fix at versions 102 and 223, on waveformrs's suite of 32 binaries (tests/test_xpnr_prescriptions.py; waveformrs itself keeps version 330 for IMRPhenomXPNR, so the reference is a patched LAL, scripts/xpnr_prescriptions_reference.py), the mismatches are 10−16–10−10 for most binaries, closer than the SpinTaylor model, whose solution of the precession equations differs from LAL's. The exceptions are the choices the model makes continuous: in-plane spins below 10−6 (up to 0.09, where LAL's 223 with aligned spins is not even the limit of its own waveforms), the edge of the PNR transition region at q = 12 (up to 1.6×10−3), the window of the PNR β at small in-plane spins (~4×10−7), the two-spin construction for every binary (3×10−8), and one binary (5×10−4 for NNLO) where the extrema of the merger-ringdown β are complex: LAL's arithmetic gives NaN there and drops the connection of β, which moves the frequency map of the higher modes, while the model takes the finite limit, as the SpinTaylor model does. And for three heavy binaries (250–480 M☉) the NNLO model differs from LAL by mismatches of 2×10−6–3.5×10−5 because LAL's uniform grid for the NNLO angles is too coarse for its splines: made 4 or 16 times finer it moves by the same amounts, to within 5×10−8 of the model's grid.

The MSA β as the in-plane spins vanish. LAL's two-spin MSA β is cos−1 of (J2 + L2 − S2)/(2JL), with |S|2 oscillating between two roots of a cubic. It does not vanish with the in-plane spins, for two reasons. Where the two roots nearly coincide, the rounding of the coefficients of the cubic moves them by ~√ε of its scale, and β by up to ~10−4 rad, a step that does not shrink with the in-plane spins. LAL perturbs exactly degenerate roots (a spin along L) by 10−9, which leaves β ≈ 3×10−5. And for binaries whose heavier spin points along L and lighter one against it, the third root rises through the other two late in the inspiral (the up-down instability): the MSA lets the oscillation span a range of |S|2 that does not shrink with the in-plane spins, as if the instability grew at once from any misalignment, and β tends to ~0.1 rad. The model takes the β of a total spin of constant magnitude, atan2(|S⊥|, L + S∥), LAL's construction for degenerate roots without the perturbation, where the separation of the roots is small, and blends LAL's in across a decade of a well-conditioned estimate of it (4|S1⊥||S2⊥| relative to the scale of the cubic, vanishing as ~12δ at equal masses), in-plane spins of ~3×10−3 to 10−2 (pnr._msa_beta). Above that the model is LAL's. Below it, it departs from LAL's procedure by up to a mismatch of ~10−5 (seen nearly edge-on) by the oscillations of |S| it drops, and by up to ~2×10−3 for up-down binaries, where LAL's own waveform is discontinuous at zero in-plane spin. Both prescriptions then tend linearly to the waveform of aligned spins as the in-plane spins vanish, as all spins vanish and across equal masses, and are continuous across LAL's switches at χ⊥ = 10−3 and q = 12 (tests/test_xpnr_smooth.py). The common set-up now also takes the in-plane spins without the floor of ~10−154 of hypot2, whose square versions 102 and 223 divide by, which gave NaN derivatives for aligned spins. Their gradients agree with finite differences as the SpinTaylor model's do (tests/test_transforms_xpnr.py), and the MLX port runs them from the same generated stages, with one stage in place of the SpinTaylor solution and the set-up of version 330.

Cost. Measured as report/xpnr_cost.py measures the SpinTaylor model (64 frequencies, all modes, tracing + lowering + compilation in a fresh process without the compilation cache), on 2026-10-25 with other work on the machine (load ~4.6): the NNLO model compiles in 9.4 s, under jax.vmap in 13.4 s and its gradient in 38.3 s, the MSA model in 11.6 s, 16.8 s and 51.9 s, against 13.0 s, 19.1 s and 50.0 s for the SpinTaylor model in the same run. The MSA system, its roots and elliptic functions carry their own derivatives, so its gradient compiles no faster than the SpinTaylor model's. At the benchmark binary (8033 frequencies) they run in 3.2 ms and 5.4 ms against 7.5 ms. These are not in report/data.

A light binary. For 5 + 1.5 solar masses from 10 Hz with 1/128 Hz resolution (260,865 frequencies, all modes, the same machine and load), the NNLO model runs in 53 ms, of which its angles take ~4 ms: its grid of the PNR interpolants, at LAL's spacing of 0.45 Hz for the NNLO angles, has 5,380 points; the rest is shared with the other models. The MSA model's grid follows the two-spin oscillations at LAL's floor of 0.01 Hz, 244,000 points, at each of which it evaluates the MSA angles, and its antisymmetric (2,2) mode evaluates the MSA α at every frequency below its transition. Two changes, neither of which changes the waveform beyond rounding (3×10−14), take it from 248 ms to ~220 ms: the MSA angles are evaluated once per point of the grid, for α, and handed to the variants of β (each computed under its own branch, which recomputed them), and the Jacobi elliptic function sn of the oscillations skips those of GSL's 15 AGM steps that no point needs (about 11), with the same arithmetic in the steps it takes. What remained, ~45 ms in the grid and ~45 ms in the antisymmetric mode, was the arithmetic of the MSA expressions, mostly their transcendental functions; the grid of nodes of the PNR interpolants (a few thousand rather than 244,000, above) and the antisymmetric α from its splines remove both: the MSA model now runs in 43 ms and the NNLO model in 41 ms (55 ms with LAL's uniform grid), on an idle machine. The phase grid (extended_phase_grid) takes the MSA model to ~117 ms at a mismatch of 1.7×10−7 from the uniform grid. For the NNLO angles it started at LAL's coarse spacing, across which the NNLO α advances by tens of radians at low frequencies, more than the interpolants of the unit vector of L can follow (a mismatch of 0.38 for this binary, without an error flag); it now starts at a spacing over which α advances by at most 0.25 rad (mismatches of 10−10–10−9 from the uniform grid), and costs more than the uniform grid for these angles.

Branches and loops under jax.vmap

Where LAL's code branches on the binary, both branches are evaluated with safe arguments and selected, or, for expensive branches, computed under the model's own switch, cond and while_loop (_transforms.py). Under jax.vmap, lax.cond with a batched predicate would compute every branch for every binary; these have batching rules instead: a branch is computed for the whole batch only if some binary in the batch takes it, and a loop runs to the longest binary in the batch while selecting only its small carry at each iteration. The three variants of the PNR β (in the calibration region with the merger-ringdown β attached, in the transition region, and without it) are such branches.

The antisymmetric amplitude

LAL smooths the amplitude ratio of the antisymmetric mode with an 81-point moving average computed in place, so each window reads averages it has already written. That is an order-40 linear recurrence, part of the definition of the waveform, which the model keeps. It is evaluated in blocks of 40 values with a triangular solve per block, inside a lax.scan; on a uniform frequency grid one system serves all blocks.

Derivatives in forward mode

Loops whose number of iterations is traced, such as the SpinTaylor integration, can be differentiated in forward mode but not in reverse mode. Reverse mode through the model's many scalar set-up computations also compiles slowly. So _transforms.differentiable makes the model a jax.custom_jvp function. Its rule computes the Jacobian with respect to the parameters that have non-zero tangents, one jax.jvp per parameter, and contracts it with the tangents. The contraction is linear, so jax.grad, which transposes it, works as well as jax.jvp and jax.jacfwd. The Jacobian is itself a custom_vmap function, so under jax.vmap of a gradient the binaries are batched inside the differentiation, four at a time (sixteen for the waveform itself: memory).

History: reproducing LAL's numerics

The version at the tag lal-faithful (commit 0bbd3c5) reproduced LAL's numerical procedure, with the SpinTaylor angles alone by default and LAL's fallbacks (version_fallback), the released model without the parity fix (pnr_parity_fix), the extended angles (extended_spintaylor) and the present model (lean) as options. Its forward and fine SpinTaylor runs repeated GSL's adaptive RKF45 steps one for one in a while_loop, with GSL's step control, LAL's retries and its Hermite-like interpolant, so that each run ended where LAL's ends, with the same samples; the backward run was the dense Dormand–Prince solution; and its splines had as many knots as LAL has samples, evaluated from the 34 knots around each point beyond 8192 knots. It reproduced GSL's Jacobi elliptic functions and elliptic integrals for the MSA angles, GSL's balanced companion matrix and Francis QR iteration for CrossZeroP5 (testing every root with its own parts, where LAL reads shifted entries), LAL's NaN arithmetic in the connection of the PNR β (a first pass with LAL's arithmetic under stop_gradient to take the decisions, a second with finite stand-ins following them), and LAL's finite differences in the higher modes, whose tangents were those of the exact derivatives. Its four prescriptions were branches of the model's switch, versions 102 and 223 of the PNR machinery sharing one branch. It agreed with LAL 6.2.0 to ~1e-5 (relative error over 200 random binaries, mismatch below ~1e-9): the (2,2) mode to ~1e-9 and the higher modes to the rounding of 1e-16 amplified by LAL's finite differences. Making it compile and run faster took three rounds of work: forward-mode derivatives, the batch-aware branches and loops, one evaluation of the SpinTaylor splines for all the points the set-up reads, the coefficients of the four higher modes under one jax.vmap, optimization barriers against XLA's duplication of shared computations, and loop bodies kept within the limits under which XLA's CPU runtime runs them on the calling thread (_math.isolated_step). The model kept every one of these.

A reference frequency scaled by the mass

LAL gives the spins, the inclination and φref at a reference frequency fref in Hz, by default the first frequency, and requires fmin ≤ fref ≤ fmax for the PNR angles. The SpinTaylor angles are needed over the band of (2,2)-equivalent frequencies 2f/m′ of the modes, and LAL starts that band at 2fmin/MMAX, with MMAX the largest m′ requested. For the (2,1) mode alone the band starts near 2fmin, above a reference at fmin: LAL leaves the SpinTaylor angles (for the MSA angles) and the model gives NaN. Of 256 random binaries (q ≤ 30, 3–400 solar masses, spins ≤ 0.99, bands from 10–30 Hz), the model with fref = fmin is finite for 4 with the (2,1) mode alone and 256 with all modes.

The option reference_mf gives the binary at fref = reference_mf/M instead, the same point of the inspiral for every binary; Binary.f_ref must then be zero (the public functions raise an error otherwise, the pure functions give NaN, and the derivative with respect to it is zero). The recommended value, REFERENCE_MF = 0.01, lies before the end of the inspiral for every binary: with the spins given there, the PN energy test ends the inspiral at Mf ≥ 0.015 for q ≤ 30 and spins ≤ 0.99 (the worst case: q = 30, both spins −0.99 along L), so the reference is at most 2/3 of the way there, below the hand-off of the SpinTaylor α to its merger-ringdown continuation at 0.97 of it. What changes (pnr.common_setup, pnr.interpolation_grid):

  • The band of the angles reaches the reference. All quantities are (2,2)-equivalent frequencies, and the minimum is taken after the scaling by 2/m′. The grid of the PNR interpolants holds the bands of the requested modes only (LAL's always holds the (2,2) band, below the integration where m′ = 1 alone), extended by soft minima and maxima (C2 blends a node wide) to hold fref with at least four nodes on each side; for heavy binaries, whose spacing is a sizeable fraction of the frequency, the spacing is at most fref/8. The integration starts below min(LAL's start, 0.25 fref), again by a soft minimum, and at 0.25 fref where LAL's start is not positive (heavy binaries with m′ = 1 or 3 alone).
  • Everything else follows from fref. The phase alignment of the (2,2) mode, the line of sight, the J frame, the offsets of α and ε and the PNR splines at the reference read the waveform struct's fref, so they move with it. The model in LAL's convention is unchanged (static branches).

With the mass-scaled reference the model is finite for at least 256 of the 256 binaries for every mode array ((2,1), (3,2), (3,3), (4,4) alone, and all modes), and its gradients are finite in float32 and float64. Along the total mass across the masses where fref crosses the lower end of the band (~200 solar masses for all modes at fmin = 20 Hz, ~50 for the (2,1) mode alone) the overlap shows no jump (fifth differences within 8 times their noise) and the forward-mode derivative agrees with finite differences to 2×10−3. In float32 the waveform agrees with float64 to mismatches below 3×10−6, and the gradients are as close to float64 as with LAL's convention. Compilation is unchanged (12.7 s against 12.9 s, cache off), and the run time is within ~10% up to 150 solar masses (at 10, 60 and 150: 21.16 ms, 6.52 ms, 6.18 ms against 21.10 ms, 6.74 ms, 5.74 ms); heavier binaries, whose reference lies below the band, integrate further down and interpolate on a finer grid (6.56 ms against 5.05 ms at 400 solar masses). The MLX port takes the option too and agrees with JAX float64 as closely as JAX float32 does.

Converters. to_mass_scaled_reference and to_physical_reference (models/xpnr/reference.py) map a binary between the conventions by integrating the model's SpinTaylor equations between the two references (4096 DOP853 steps in u = 1/(Mω)), as pure functions (jit, vmap, grad). They keep the spins, L and the line of sight. The frame at the new reference is the one the equations carry along, turned about L by the orbital phase between the references, taken from IMRPhenomXAS's (2,2) phase, by which the model's alignment of the phase moves. With that turn, aligned-spin binaries give the same waveform in both conventions (mismatch ~10−11 without maximisation), and round trips return the binary to ~10−14. The orbital phase is thousands of radians for light binaries, so in float32 the converted spins and angles are good to ~10−4: convert in float64. The basis of the polarizations follows L at the reference, so the two descriptions differ by a rotation of the polarizations, which the converters return.

For precessing binaries the conventions differ by more than numerics, because IMRPhenomXPNR itself depends on where the reference lies: it takes the aligned spins of the co-precessing modes, J and the parameters of its fits at the reference. Over – binaries drawn at 20 Hz (q ≤ 12, 20–90 solar masses, spins ≤ 0.9) and converted to 0.01/M (inside the band), LAL with the parity fix at the two references differs by a mismatch of median – and maximum – (above 10−3 for –), maximised over time and phase. The model with the mass-scaled reference agrees with LAL given the converted binary at fref = 0.01/M to a median of – and a maximum of –, as closely as the model agrees with LAL in its own convention. As the in-plane spins vanish, the waveforms of the two conventions converge (mismatch 4×10−5 at χp = 0.3, 10−8 at 0.01). A converted binary therefore stands for the same physical system, but a waveform model whose reference moves is a different waveform for precessing binaries, by as much as LAL's own dependence on fref.

A reference-invariant model. The model with the mass-scaled reference removes that dependence: whatever its reference_mf, it first carries the binary to the canonical reference REFERENCE_MF = 0.01 (reference.to_canonical, the converter's integration with a step count fixed by the two references, so the MLX port takes it too), and the rest of the model runs as at 0.01. The aligned spins of the co-precessing modes, J, the parameters of the PNR fits, the offsets of the angles, the alignment of the phase, the line of sight and the basis of the polarizations are then all taken at 0.01. Reading the state at 0.01 from the model's own SpinTaylor solution instead would be circular, since the start and end of that integration and the grid of the PNR interpolants follow from the aligned spins at the reference. The converters pass through 0.01 too, with the orbital phase of both legs from the aligned spins there, so conversions compose. A binary converted between two references (0.006 and 0.012, 0.01 and 0.008, 0.01 and 0.002; precessing, nearly aligned and aligned) gives the same waveform without any maximisation, to a largest relative difference of 10−13 (10−7 at q = 18, whose integration takes few steps), in float64, float32 and MLX; without the conversion the waveform changes by order one. Before this, the model at two references differed by mismatches of 10−5–2×10−2, as LAL does. Two checks show that this is the model's construction and not the converter. The converter's evolved spins, L and E1 agree with the model's own SpinTaylor solution to 10−13 (10−8 for q = 18), and with LAL's SimInspiralSpinTaylorPNEvolveOrbit to 10−9. With reference_mf = 0.01 nothing changes. Another value costs an integration between the two references and a compilation of its own (the option is static): 14.3 s and 14.4 s to compile at 0.006 and 0.002 against 12.7 s, and at 10, 60 and 400 solar masses 21.73 ms, 6.69 ms, 7.03 ms at 0.006 and 23.19 ms, 8.44 ms, 8.80 ms at 0.002. For a reference given in Hz, to_mass_scaled_reference followed by the model at 0.01 gives the same waveform without recompiling.

Against LAL. The invariant model is compared with LAL where LAL's reference is the canonical one, fref = 0.01/M: there both take everything at the same point, and they agree to a median of – and a maximum of – (above). Elsewhere the difference is LAL's own dependence on its reference. With the same spins and angles given to LAL at fref = Mnear/M, off 0.01 by a relative offset drawn log-uniformly in [0.2%, 20%], the mismatch with the invariant model has median – and maximum – for offsets up to 2% (– binaries), – and – for 2–5% (–), and – and – for 5–20% (–). From LAL's test point at 20 Hz it is median – and maximum – (– of – above 10−3), as LAL at 20 Hz against itself at 0.01/M. LAL's dependence on its reference is not always smooth either: for one binary at q = 11 its mismatch with the invariant model rises from 3×10−7 to 5×10−5 and falls back to 2×10−6 as its reference moves from 24.04 to 24.22 to 24.28 Hz.

Single precision

IMRPhenomXPNR is evaluated in the precision of the binary's parameters, as NRSur7dq4 is, and returns the strain times 1021 unless scaled_strain=False. The comparisons with LAL use scaled_strain=False. A float32 binary is evaluated without promoting any float32 value to float64. Python constants in LAL's expressions remain weakly typed float64 scalars, which take float32 when they meet the data; tests/test_xpnr_float32.py checks the waveform, its forward derivative and its gradient. The changes that made float32 work change the float64 waveform by rounding only:

  • No SI round trips. The masses stay in solar masses in both precisions (at the tag, float64 passed them through kilograms as LAL does; in float32 XLA folds the square of the solar mass in kilograms into the derivatives, as it did for NRSur7dq4). The amplitude takes the distance in Mpc. Two expressions of LAL that XLA rewrites into powers beyond float32's range are written another way: the velocity of the chirp-time bound, where XLA turned a division by c into a multiplication by c8, and the arctan window of the PNR β, whose 500th powers overflow. The 500th power in that window, below 1.5 for β in [0, 0.01], exceeds float32's range where the ringdown β of the fits is below −0.3 rad (large mass ratios with in-plane spins): its value is finite but its derivative was NaN, so there it is computed as π/2 − arctan of the inverse power, the same function to rounding. The pseudo-PN coefficients of the higher-mode inspiral amplitudes divide by a product of powers of the collocation frequencies, ~10−19 for the (2,1) mode at Mf ~ 0.01, whose square in the derivative of the quotient falls below float32's range (NaN derivatives of the (2,1) mode, mostly above q = 10); the coefficients do not depend on the scale of the frequencies, and are computed in units of the end of the inspiral.
  • Constants of the precision. Machine epsilon, the smallest normal number and the floors of safe square roots (10−300 in float64) follow the precision. The higher modes take exact derivatives instead of LAL's finite differences with a step of 10−9.
  • Types from the binary. Default float and integer types, numpy coefficient tables and the counts of loops follow the precision (_math.real, _math.integer), since an int64 count or a float64 table promotes float32 data to float64.

The model in float32 agrees with its float64 waveform to a mismatch of ~10−9–3×10−7 for the binaries tried, and its gradients agree to ~10−4. Its derivatives are finite in float32 (over 300 random binaries up to q = 30, each mode and the polarizations; before two fixes described above, the derivative of the (2,1) mode was NaN for half of them, mostly above q = 10, and that of every mode for 2%). The version at the tag, which replayed LAL's adaptive RKF45 steps (at tolerances raised to 10−5 in float32) and sampled them on LAL's time lattice, differed from float64 by mismatches of 10−5–10−2. Running JAX's float64 switch off inside a trace (jax.enable_x64(False)) would have avoided most of the type changes, but JAX caches the conversions of numpy constants and the executables of eager operations without regard to that switch, so a float32 evaluation corrupted later float64 ones in the same process.

On Apple GPUs with MLX

waveformx.models.xpnr.mlx runs the model in MLX on the GPU of Apple silicon, in float32: polarizations(binary, frequencies) for one binary or a batch (shared or per-binary frequencies), differentiable with mx.grad, and polarizations_and_jacobian. It is not a second implementation of the model. The model is cut into stages that are functions of one binary's scalars (the set-up: four stages of 7–41 thousand scalar operations) or of its scalars and one point (the PNR angles at a grid point, the angles, modes and twist at a frequency). Each stage is traced to a jaxpr in float32 and lowered (mlx/_program.py) to straight-line code on scalars: arrays of static shape become arrays of scalars, loops of static length are unrolled, branches are computed both ways and selected, the small linear solves (LAPACK's getrf with partial pivoting) and sorts are written out, complex numbers become pairs, and what does not depend on the inputs is computed once. The code runs as a Metal kernel over the batch and the points, its derivatives from the JVP that JAX writes, one thread per binary, point and direction. Products added once are fused into multiply-adds, as XLA's CPU backend fuses them: without this, the float32 fits of the PNR β and the collocation solves of the higher modes came out 20–100 times further from float64 than JAX's float32. The rest is written for MLX: the SpinTaylor integration (a Metal kernel, one SIMD group per binary with a lane per component of the state, calling the translated derivatives), the searches in its dense output, the natural splines of the PNR interpolants (the inverse of the tridiagonal matrix as a filter of the second differences), Boole's integral of γ and the moving average of the antisymmetric mode.

The derivatives are forward-mode Jacobians with respect to the twelve parameters, contracted with the cotangents in a custom VJP, as the JAX model contracts its own. One choice differs from JAX's float32: the Newton step at the end of the inspiral takes the exact derivative of the stopping conditions in time, where JAX takes the secant of the bisection's bracket, which float32 does not resolve. JAX's float32 derivative of the end of the inspiral is off by up to 90% for the binaries tried; the MLX one agrees with float64 to ~10−6.

Over 200 random binaries of report/extended (1024 frequencies each), the weighted mismatch of the MLX waveform against JAX's float64 waveform has median 6.4×10-9, 90th percentile 5.2×10-7 and maximum 1.1×10-2; JAX's float32 waveform: 1.1×10-8, 3.1×10-7 and 1.1×10-2. 14 binaries exceed 10−6 in MLX and 9 in JAX's float32; the largest is the same binary of 411 M☉ for both (JAX float32: 1.1×10-2). IMRPhenomXAS

IMRPhenomXAS, the aligned-spin (2,2) mode of IMRPhenomX, is a public approximant ("IMRPhenomXAS") and has an MLX version (waveformx.models.xas.mlx). Both are written for the cases it is mostly called in: non-uniform grids of up to about a thousand frequencies, often many binaries at once, and long uniform grids.

A table per binary, a short formula per frequency

The coefficients come from IMRPhenomXPNR's (2,2) mode (models/xpnr/internals.py, amp22.py, phase22.py), so the fits, collocation systems and connections are LAL's. models/xas/core.py then rewrites the model in powers of the frequency in Hz. In each of the three regions of the phase, LAL's sum of powers of Mf and their products with log Mf becomes a sum over 16 powers of f (at most 13 per region), with the powers of M folded into the coefficients and log Mf = log M + log f. LAL's time shift and its reference phase (but for the part of the leading term, below) fold into the same coefficients. The amplitude becomes a polynomial in f1/3, a rational function and an exponential. Each binary then has a table of 86 numbers, and a frequency needs only its own powers. In a batch with shared frequencies (jax.vmap) those powers are computed once, and per binary and frequency what is left is one region's sum, the arctangent and exponential of the merger, and a sine and cosine. The JAX model is one elementwise expression with the regions selected, without the stacked arrays and reductions of a first version, which took twice as long on long grids. Its derivatives are JAX's own, in either mode.

Three changes from LAL's arithmetic make float32 and float64 compute the same thing. Both work in solar masses rather than LAL's round trip through kilograms. LAL's quasi-normal-mode fits are ratios of polynomials in the final spin whose numerator and denominator both nearly vanish at af = 1. In float32 that cancellation loses a part in 104 of the ringdown frequency at af = 0.9 and up to 1% near 1, so the fits are evaluated in powers of 1 − af, with coefficients re-expanded in float64. In float64 they differ from LAL's form by up to 10−11, LAL's own rounding. The third change, the leading term of the phase, is described below. One fix reaches IMRPhenomXPNR too: the peak frequency of the ringdown amplitude took sqrt(1 − γ22) in the branch not taken for γ2 > 1. That gave NaN reverse-mode gradients (XPNR's forward-mode Jacobians did not see it), and the square root is now guarded as the others are, in scripts/generate_fits.py.

float32 and binary neutron stars

From 20 Hz the inspiral phase of a binary neutron star reaches ~104 radians, where one float32 ulp is ~10−3 radians. Summing the phase in float32 lost about ten ulps, from the rounding of the coefficient of f−5/3, of the reference phase that cancels it and of the power itself. Light binaries then had mismatches of 10−5–10−4 against float64. The leading term is now separate. Its coefficient is T0 = K0 M1/3 / (m1 m2), its value at the reference frequency is A, and T0 f−5/3 − A, its sum with the rest and the reduction to [−π, π] are computed in extended precision. JAX uses float64, which the package always enables; Metal uses double-float pairs of floats, with sums and products made exact by fused multiply-adds. Every other term stays below ~103 radians and is computed in the working precision, as are the derivatives.

Against float64 at the same float32-rounded inputs, the weighted mismatch (aLIGO design, the larger of the polarizations) over 300 random binaries of 2–500 M☉ from 20 Hz has median 9.4×10-10 and maximum 1.3×10-7 in JAX's float32, and 1.2×10-9 and 3.2×10-7 in MLX. Over 100 binary neutron stars (1–2.5 M☉, spins up to 0.6) on 1000 frequencies from 20 Hz, the maxima are 3.4×10-8 (JAX) and 7.4×10-8 (MLX). On a uniform grid of 1/256 Hz to 2048 Hz (half a million frequencies; 10 binaries) they are 1.8×10-8 and 2.5×10-8. The gradient of the log-likelihood of tests/test_xpnr_float32.py is within that test's bound for 29 of 30 binaries in JAX's float32 (at most 1.0×100 of the bound) and 30 in MLX (at most 7.6×10-1).

In float64, over 300 random binaries (2–500 M☉, fmin of 10–20 Hz, 1000 frequencies), the model agrees with SimInspiralChooseFDWaveformSequence to 2.4×10-10 at most (median 8.8×10-13), with mismatches below 4.4×10-16.

On Apple GPUs with MLX

A call is two Metal kernels. The set-up is generated from the JAX function, as for IMRPhenomXPNR (the lowering of models/xpnr/mlx/_program.py): one thread per binary, and one per binary and parameter for its forward-mode derivatives. The double-float leading term is written by hand into the same kernel. The waveform at the frequencies is written by hand, one thread per binary and frequency, with the region chosen by branching. Its derivatives along the eight parameters of an aligned-spin binary are linear in those of the table, so they cost a few multiply-adds per direction. polarizations_and_jacobian writes them out. For mx.grad, a third kernel contracts them with the cotangents and sums over the frequencies inside the kernel, so the Jacobian is never stored.

The set-up of one binary runs on one GPU thread, where a precise division takes ~240 ns and an exponential ~500 ns. It uses Metal's fast division, exponential and logarithm, which halves its time without changing the accuracy above. With MLX's ~0.2 ms to submit and wait for the GPU, one binary takes 482 µs, against 150 µs for JAX on the CPU and 84 µs for LALSimulation. MLX pays off with size. One binary on 220 frequencies takes 566 µs (16 binaries 1.72 ms), and batches reach 5,098,279 waveforms of 1000 frequencies per second at 4096 binaries per call, 17.6× JAX's best on the CPU. In a new process, importing takes 528 ms, the first waveform 607 ms (tracing the set-up and compiling the kernels) and the first gradient 20.18 ms.

IMRPhenomXHM

IMRPhenomXHM, IMRPhenomXAS with the (2,|1|), (3,|3|), (3,|2|) and (4,|4|) modes, is a public approximant ("IMRPhenomXHM") with an MLX version (waveformx.models.xhm.mlx), written as IMRPhenomXAS is: tables per binary, a short formula per frequency. It returns the spherical-harmonic modes as well as the polarizations.

One code path

Where LALSimulation offers several versions, only its defaults are implemented: the 122022 release with recalibrated amplitudes (PhenomXHMReleaseVersion; IMRPhenomXPNR uses 122019), the default inspiral, intermediate and ringdown versions of each mode, and no multibanding (LAL's default for uniform grids, which interpolates the modes to ~10−4). The modes are those of the polarizations (IMRPhenomXHM_MultiMode2): the (3,2) mode recycles the (2,2) mode in its ringdown and the higher modes are not cut at 0.3/M, where LAL's one-mode functions differ. Mode arrays are not supported. The 122022 amplitudes are new code (their fits generated from waveformrs into _generated/hm_amp_fits_*.py); the phases reuse the higher-mode code of IMRPhenomXPNR (models/xhm/reference.py is LAL's computation in Mf, core.py the tables).

Four changes from LAL keep float32 and float64 one computation. The derivatives LAL takes by finite differences (the inspiral and ringdown amplitudes at the boundaries of the intermediate region, with a step of 10−9; the first and second derivatives of the rotated (3,2) ringdown phase that fix its intermediate phase, with a step of 10−7) are exact. LAL's second difference amplifies rounding by ~1014, so its (3,2) differs from the exact one by a mismatch of 8.0×10-10 (median; at most 5.5×10-6); waveformrs reproduces LAL's values bit for bit, so they are LAL's. The QNM and mixing fits of the higher modes are in powers of 1 − af, as for the (2,2), and the mixing coefficients are kept in the working precision (LAL rounds them to single precision, which made the waveform a staircase in the parameters). Two ill-conditioned collocation systems, the intermediate amplitude (up to eight coefficients) and the intermediate phase of the (3,2) (a quartic in 1/f), are solved for the same polynomials in variables centred on their regions. The pseudo-PN amplitude coefficients come from the inverse of their constant system rather than LAL's closed form, whose small products underflow in the derivatives in float32.

The phase of every mode is that of the (2,2) rescaled in the inspiral, aligned with it at low frequency, and in the ringdown of the (3,2) a rotation of the (2,2) and the spheroidal (3,2). The large reference phase of the (2,2), ~104 radians for binary neutron stars, enters all of them through that alignment and rotation; it is taken out exactly ((m/2) T0 (2f/m)−5/3 is the leading term of mode m), so each mode is (m/2)8/3 T0 f−5/3 − (m/2) A plus a phase of at most ~103 radians, and the first part is computed in extended precision as for the (2,2).

Accuracy

In float64, over 200 random binaries (as for IMRPhenomXAS, frequencies below 0.3/M), the polarizations agree with SimInspiralChooseFDWaveformSequence to 2.1×10-4 at most (median 1.5×10-7) and mismatches below 5.1×10-8, the difference being the (3,2) mode's; the (2,2), (2,1), (3,3) and (4,4) modes agree with LAL's one-mode functions to 3.0×10-8. In float32, against float64 at the same inputs, the weighted mismatch of the polarizations over 200 random binaries from 20 Hz is at most 1.3×10-7 in JAX and 3.2×10-7 in MLX, and of the worst mode 5.6×10-7 and 1.3×10-6; over 60 binary neutron stars at most 3.3×10-8 and 7.3×10-8 (worst mode 1.9×10-7, 3.5×10-7), and on a uniform grid of 1/256 Hz 1.9×10-9 and 3.4×10-9. Near maximal spin the (3,2) mode alone loses more in float32 (~10−4 at q = 25, χ = 0.995): the second derivative of the (2,2) ringdown phase that fixes its intermediate phase comes from IMRPhenomXPNR's collocation of the (2,2) ringdown, ill-conditioned in float32. The gradient of the log-likelihood of tests/test_xpnr_float32.py is within that test's bound for 20 of 20 binaries in JAX's float32 (at most 1.5×10-1 of it) and 20 in MLX (at most 2.1×10-1).

The mass-scaled reference

IMRPhenomXAS and IMRPhenomXHM take reference_mf as the other models do: phi_ref is given at fref = reference_mf/M, Binary.f_ref must be zero (NaN otherwise, with a zero derivative with respect to it). The models replace fref by reference_mf/M before the table is made, so its dependence on the masses enters the derivatives; in MLX the set-up kernels take it as their input, and the Jacobian adds its column to those of the masses. The converters (models/xas/reference.py, for both models, and in MLX lowered from them) move phi_ref by half the change of the (2,2) phase (LAL's phase_22/η + linb Mf) between the references. IMRPhenomXHM aligns its higher modes with the (2,2) at the reference, so mode m turns by m/2 times that change: phi_ref is kept modulo 2π, not π, which would negate the (2,1) and (3,3) modes. The converted binary gives every mode and the polarizations to rounding (largest relative difference ~10−12 for binary neutron stars, tests/test_xas.py, tests/test_xhm.py), and for aligned spins IMRPhenomXPNR's converters give the same phi_ref to 10−8.

On Apple GPUs with MLX

The set-up is five generated kernels run side by side, one for the (2,2) and the factors of the modes, one per higher mode. The waveform at the frequencies is a kernel written by hand that calls a function generated from each mode's formula, with the leading terms in double-float. The Jacobian takes the forward-mode derivatives of the tables and of these functions, eight directions at once. The gradient is reverse mode: the cotangent of each mode's table summed over the frequencies in the kernel, then contracted with the table's Jacobian. That costs about ten times the waveform, where forward mode cost fifty times. TaylorF2 and IMRPhenomD

TaylorF2 and IMRPhenomD are public approximants ("TaylorF2", "IMRPhenomD") with MLX versions (waveformx.models.taylorf2.mlx, waveformx.models.phenomd.mlx), written as IMRPhenomXAS is: a table of coefficients per binary, a short formula per frequency, the leading term of the inspiral phase in extended precision. They are ported from waveformrs (src/taylor.rs, src/pn.rs, src/imrphenomd/) and checked against LAL.

TaylorF2 has LAL's defaults: the 3.5PN phase with every aligned-spin term, the leading-order amplitude and no tidal terms. Its phase is LAL's sum over powers of v = (πMf)1/3 and their products with log v, written in powers of f in Hz (eight terms but for the leading one) with the powers of M folded into a table of 15 numbers. Its post-Newtonian phasing (models/taylorf2/pn.py) is shared with IMRPhenomD. As in LAL's SimInspiralChooseFDWaveformSequence, every frequency is evaluated, without the cut at the innermost stable circular orbit of LAL's uniform grid, and f_ref = 0 refers the phase to coalescence. Over 300 random binaries (as for IMRPhenomXAS, to the Schwarzschild ISCO or 2048 Hz) it agrees with LAL to 3.8×10-10 at most (median 1.6×10-12), mismatches below 4.4×10-16: rounding, mostly of the ~104 radians of the phase of light binaries.

IMRPhenomD has three regions each in its phase and amplitude. The inspiral phase is TaylorF2's (without the 3PN spin-spin term, which IMRPhenomD was not calibrated with) and four pseudo-PN terms; the intermediate phase is a sum of powers and a logarithm; the merger-ringdown phase adds an arctangent. The amplitude is a polynomial in f1/3, a quartic and a Lorentzian times an exponential. The table has 75 numbers. Three things differ from LAL. The masses are in solar masses and are not reordered (the fits are symmetric under the exchange of the bodies), so the waveform is smooth through equal masses. The quartic of the intermediate amplitude is solved for in powers of Mf/Mfpeak, better conditioned in float32. And the quasi-normal-mode frequencies come from closed-form fits (below). Where LAL drops a square root of the peak frequency (γ2 > 1), its argument is kept positive in the branch not taken, so that the derivatives stay finite.

The quasi-normal-mode frequencies

LAL interpolates the ringdown and damping frequencies of the (2,2) mode in the final spin a with a natural cubic spline through 1003 tabulated values (QNMData_a, QNMData_fring, QNMData_fdamp, given to six or seven digits). waveformx replaces the spline with Chebyshev series of degree 20 in ((1 − a)/2)1/3: the frequencies vary like powers of 1 − a near a = 1, where the damping frequency vanishes, and the cube root makes them smooth. Rational functions in the same variable and in (1 − a)1/2 were tried too; their fits were less accurate and some had poles in the range. scripts/fit_phenomd_qnm.py fits the series to the relative error, with weights reduced for points far off the others, and writes models/phenomd/_qnm_fit.py. The error of LAL's spline is estimated by leaving out each point of the table and evaluating the spline through the others there. That is the interpolation error at twice the spacing, an overestimate where the frequencies are smooth, together with the noise of the table, which the spline passes through.

Single precision and derivatives

NRSur7dq4

NRSur7dq4 interpolates numerical-relativity waveforms of precessing binaries. It was trained on mass ratios up to 4 and spin magnitudes up to 0.8, and LAL allows it to extrapolate beyond those. It integrates an ODE for the coprecessing frame, the orbital phase and the spins, with time derivatives given by fits at 233 nodes. It then evaluates the coorbital modes from fits at their empirical-interpolation nodes and rotates them to the inertial frame. The JAX version follows LALSimIMRPrecessingNRSur.c and waveformrs's translation of it.

  • Data. The 17 MB HDF5 file is read once with h5py. Every dynamics fit becomes a dense row over the union of the monomials the fits use, so the nine fits of a node are one matrix–vector product. The fits of the waveform nodes are stored by the order of their mass-ratio monomial, so the mass ratio, which is the same at every node, is folded into the coefficients once per binary.
  • Dynamics. LAL steps from the reference frequency to the nearest node, takes three RK4 steps (forwards or backwards, depending on that node), and covers the remaining nodes with fourth-order Adams–Bashforth steps in both directions. Since the starting node depends on the binary, the integration is one lax.scan of fixed length. It walks backwards from the start and then forwards, evaluating the fits of one node per step as LAL does. The Adams–Bashforth weights depend only on the node and are tabulated once.
  • Splines. GSL's natural cubic splines solve a tridiagonal system whose matrix depends only on the knots. As in waveformrs, it is factorised once when the data are read, so fitting a spline needs only the two substitution sweeps.
  • Frame rotation. LAL's Wigner D-matrices use negative powers of the quaternion's components, with special cases where they vanish, as they do for spins along the orbital angular momentum. Here the negative powers are cancelled analytically, which makes each element a polynomial in the quaternion: the same values, no special cases, and finite gradients in the non-precessing limit.
  • Output grid. The number of samples depends on when the orbital frequency reaches π fmin, so it is found on the host. A compiled pass evaluates the frequency fits, and numpy repeats LAL's search operation by operation. The waveform is then computed on a power-of-two buffer of at least 256 samples, which waveforms of similar length share, and trimmed to the binary's length.

polarizations_at(binary, times) evaluates the waveform at any times relative to the peak. It is pure and differentiable with respect to every parameter, including f_ref, which moves the reference time and so the frame. It is what jax.vmap, jax.grad and the conversions use.

The mass-scaled reference

With reference_mf the binary is given where the orbital frequency of the surrogate is Mωref = π reference_mf, whatever the mass (f_ref zero, NaN otherwise with a zero derivative). The reference time on the dynamics grid then depends on the mass ratio and the spins only, through LAL's interpolation of the frequency fits, which the pure function already traces; no host search is needed and the masses no longer move the reference. REFERENCE_MF = 0.01 (Mω = 0.0314) lies inside the surrogate for every binary it covers: over 24000 binaries with q ≤ 6 and |χ| ≤ 0.8 the surrogate starts at Mω ≤ 0.0224 (q = 6, both spins 0.8 along L; 0.0202 for q ≤ 4), so values of reference_mf down to ~0.0072 work across that range; the public functions raise below the start of a binary's surrogate, and allow a reference below fmin.

The converters (models/nrsur7dq4/reference.py, and in MLX) integrate the surrogate's dynamics from the old reference as the model does, take the state where the orbital frequency is the new one, and give the spins, the inclination and phi_ref in the coorbital frame there, with the angle by which the polarization basis turns (as IMRPhenomXPNR's converters). The time of the new reference follows the model's own rule, the frequency fits at the nodes for the spins at the reference, so it depends on the converted spins. The model starts its integration with an Euler step from the reference to the nearest node, whose first-order error depends on where the reference falls between the nodes (up to a few per cent of the waveform); the converted state is chosen so that this step lands on the old integration's state at that node. Six fixed-point iterations find the time and the state together. What remains is the surrogate's own dependence on where its reference lies: its RK4 start and Adams–Bashforth steps from two different nodes give orbital phases that drift apart by up to ~10−2 radians over the waveform. A converted binary therefore gives the same waveform up to the rotation of the polarization basis and that drift: a largest relative difference of 10−4–2×10−2 and mismatches, without any maximisation, below 10−4; a round trip, which integrates twice, returns the spins to ~10−2 at worst. The converters are pure (jax.jit, jax.vmap, jax.grad, float32); in MLX they agree with JAX float64 to 3×10−6 in the spins, with gradients to 10−5.

Single precision and the scale of the output

NRSur7dq4 is evaluated in the precision of the binary's parameters. A Binary of float32 arrays is evaluated in float32 throughout, with a float32 copy of the data cast from the float64 arrays (the spline factorisations included). A test checks that no operation of the traced waveform, or of its gradient, is in float64. The public functions take Python numbers, so they stay in float64. Two changes make float32 usable:

  • The output is the strain times 1021. This applies to every NRSur7dq4 function, the public functions and the conversions included, unless scaled_strain=False. The strain itself fits in float32, but its square (~10−42) and a detector's power spectral density (~10−47) do not. The comparisons with LAL in this report use scaled_strain=False.
  • Nothing passes through SI units. LAL converts the masses to kilograms and back (m * MSUN_SI / MSUN_SI) and works with the distance in metres. In float32, XLA folded the square of the solar mass in kilograms (~4×1060, beyond float32's range) into the derivative of the mass ratio, so the compiled gradient was NaN. The derivative with respect to a distance of ~1025 m underflowed to zero. The masses now stay in solar masses, and the amplitude is one constant times the total mass over the distance in Mpc. In float64 this changes the waveform by rounding only.

What float32 costs in accuracy was measured on 300 pairs of binaries, drawn as for the accuracy comparison (report/float32_nrsur7dq4.py). 150 pairs are two independent binaries, whose overlaps are near zero. The other 150 are a binary and a small perturbation of its masses, spins and angles, with a median overlap of 0.95. Both binaries of a pair are evaluated on one time grid, in float64 and in float32, projected on a detector and tapered at the start. An inner product between two different waveforms changes by 2.4×10-6 of the product of their norms (median), and by at most 1.0×10-4. Doing the inner products themselves in float32 adds at most 1.7×10-7 to the overlap. The error of a single waveform, 3.8×10-5 (median, noise-weighted), is mostly rounding noise from the final resampling onto the float32 time grid: with everything up to the polarizations on the surrogate's own times in float64, it is still 2.5×10-5. That noise is almost uncorrelated with another waveform, so it barely moves their inner product. The ODE integrated in float32 is what does. With the dynamics in float64 and the rest in float32, the change of the overlap falls from 2.1×10-6 to 2.8×10-7 (median), and from 1.9×10-5 to 1.7×10-6 at the 90th percentile.

That is 1.7×10-3 at ρ = 20 and 4.2×10-2 at ρ = 100 (2.8×10-1 at the 90th percentile). With noise in the data, a further ⟨n, δh⟩ of about ρ ‖δh‖ / ‖h‖ is added. On this CPU, float32 is 1.9× as fast for one waveform (2.78 ms against 5.34 ms on 8192 samples). It is 1.8× as fast for a batch of 64 under jax.vmap (55.69 ms against 102 ms), and 1.4× for a gradient. The dynamics, a sequence of 230 small steps, take 45% of a float64 batch. Keeping them in float64 would make the inner products about ten times more accurate, at the cost of part of the speed-up on the CPU; the package does not do this. float32 matters most on accelerators, where float64 is much slower or, as on Apple GPUs, missing. The MLX version below runs there.

On Apple GPUs with MLX

waveformx.models.nrsur7dq4.mlx is NRSur7dq4 in MLX, Apple's array library, which runs on the GPU of Apple silicon. It is an optional dependency (pip install waveformx[mlx]). The module is separate from the JAX code, which it leaves unchanged, and reads the same data through the JAX loader. It provides:

  • the pure functions polarizations_at and modes_at;
  • LAL's waveforms on its grid: td_polarizations, td_modes and batch_td_polarizations;
  • the conditioned waveforms: simulate_td, simulate_fd and their pure functions.

Everything is float32 and differentiable with mx.grad. The pure functions take one binary or a batch along a leading axis. MLX has no float64 on the GPU and no scan, while_loop or cond. Its vmap and forward-mode derivatives do not pass through custom kernels. So:

  • Splines as matrices. The JAX version fits its splines with two substitution sweeps (a lax.scan). The knots are fixed, so each spline is a fixed linear map, computed in float64 when the data are read:
    • the spline of the dynamics states, evaluated at the waveform's times, is one 2000 × 230 matrix;
    • the second derivatives of the spline through the polarizations are the inverse of its tridiagonal system applied to the right-hand side.
    For a batch, each map is one matrix product over all the binaries.
  • The dynamics in one Metal kernel. The 230 sequential RK4 and Adams–Bashforth steps run in a hand-written Metal kernel, with one SIMD group per binary and one state component per lane. A second kernel carries dual numbers through the same steps. It gives the Jacobian of the states with respect to the mass ratio, the spins and the reference time, and the kernel's VJP contracts that Jacobian.
  • The frame rotation in Metal kernels. The Wigner rotation of every mode sample is a generated kernel. Its VJP is a kernel too: it differentiates with respect to the quaternion through the generators of the rotation, using D(pq) = D(p) D(q).
  • Harmonics as Fourier series. The spin-weighted harmonics are trigonometric series in the inclination, with coefficients computed once in float64. Evaluated directly, the powers of cos(θ/2) and sin(θ/2) in the gradient were fused by mx.compile into a kernel with more arguments than Metal allows.
  • LAL's grids on the host. The lengths and epochs are computed in float64, as the JAX driver computes them. A batch is set up all at once: the frequency fits of every binary in one call, then LAL's search in numpy over the batch. This takes 2.36 ms for 1024 binaries, against ~0.15 ms per binary one by one. A binary that LAL refuses is set up again on its own, which raises LAL's error. For 1200 binaries the grids agree with the JAX driver's:
    • from 20 Hz: the same length and epoch for 407 of the 407 binaries LAL accepts, and LAL's error for 193 of the 193 it refuses;
    • from the start of the surrogate: the same length and epoch for 600 of the 600 binaries LAL accepts;
    • the start times differ by at most 1.0×10-11 M, the rounding of the fits summed in another order.
    The binaries of a batch are sorted by length and evaluated in chunks of a power-of-two size.
  • Conditioning. LAL does not high-pass filter NRSur7dq4, so SimInspiralTD and SimInspiralFD need only its start taper, the padding and an FFT. The end of the taper depends on the samples, so it is found on the host from the float32 samples.

The accuracy was measured on the same 300 pairs as JAX's float32 above, in the same way (report/mlx_nrsur7dq4.py). The MLX waveform is evaluated by polarizations_at at the float32 times.

Time and frequency domains

simulate_td and simulate_fd reproduce LALSimulation 6.2.0's SimInspiralTD and SimInspiralFD. IMRPhenomXPNR can be given in the time domain and NRSur7dq4 in the frequency domain, and each model can also be conditioned in its own domain. They follow waveformrs's src/conversions/ and src/conditioning.rs.

LAL's conditioning starts the model below the requested frequency by an amount set by bounds on the chirp time, merger time and ringdown time. It tapers the extra length, applies a Butterworth high-pass filter, shifts the waveform in time and crops it to a length and epoch derived from the same bounds. The work is split by what needs to be exact:

  • On the host, in Python floats. The lengths, shifts, taper widths and GPS epochs are computed with LAL's arithmetic. This includes C's pow, cbrt and round, and the fused multiply-adds of clang's arm64 build, reproduced exactly through rational arithmetic. As a result, lengths and epochs are LAL's bit for bit.
  • In JAX, pure and differentiable. The bounds are computed again where the waveform depends on them continuously: the starting frequency given to the model and the cutoff of the first high-pass filter. So do the filter coefficients, the filter itself (a lax.scan of LAL's recursion per second-order section), the tapers and the Fourier transforms. Arrays have fixed, power-of-two lengths, so one compilation serves many binaries.

IMRPhenomXPNR smooths its antisymmetric amplitude with a moving average over the array of frequencies, which LAL takes over its band. To keep one array length per transform length, the model is evaluated on the band, followed by frequencies just above it and then the band's last 82 frequencies again. The first L − 41 values are then averaged as LAL averages them, and the last 41 are taken from the end. simulate_td_function and simulate_fd_function return the conversion as a pure function. It keeps the lengths, shifts, tapers and epoch of a reference binary, while the starting frequencies and filter cutoffs follow the binary, so the gradient includes their dependence on the masses and spins.

Accuracy

The random draws are those of waveformrs's report, so the two reports can be compared. For IMRPhenomXPNR: total masses log-uniform in 5–300 M☉, mass ratios uniform in 1/20–1, isotropic spins of magnitude up to 0.99, fmin ∈ {10, 15, 20} Hz, fref = fmin or up to 3 fmin, and isotropic lines of sight. For NRSur7dq4: mass ratios 1–6 with either body first, total masses 20–300 M☉, sampling at 1–8 kHz, and fmin above the surrogate's start or zero.

NRSur7dq4 agrees with LAL to 2.8×10-12 at most (median 3.0×10-13), with LAL's length and epoch in every compared draw (200/200). LAL evaluates all 200 draws, and so does waveformx. The residual comes from summing the fits in a different order and from XLA's own exp, sin and cos. The rotation to the inertial frame and the splines are linear, so the errors stay at rounding level. waveformrs, which agrees with LAL bit for bit, gives the same comparison (2.8×10-12).

IMRPhenomXPNR is compared with LAL 6.2.0 with the parity fix through waveformrs, which agrees with the patched LALSimulation to ~3e-15. For the 193 draws for which LAL keeps the SpinTaylor angles, the mismatch has median 2.8×10-8 and maximum 1.7×10-4 (relative errors: median 5.0×10-5, 90th percentile 5.2×10-4, maximum 9.7×10-3). The difference is the model's: the precession equations solved by a different method and from a lower frequency, LAL's switches made continuous (the two-spin construction for every binary weighs most), exact derivatives in the higher modes. It grows with the total mass, where the merger and ringdown, and so the connection of the angles, carry more of the band: the median mismatch is 2.8×10-9 below 20 M☉ and 1.3×10-7 above 150 M☉. 1% of these draws exceed 1e-4. For the 7 draws for which LAL leaves the SpinTaylor angles (its integration fails, or the in-plane spins are tiny), the model keeps them: the mismatch has median 1.7×10-3 and maximum 2.1×10-1. Against the released LAL 6.2.0, without the parity fix, the mismatch where LAL keeps the SpinTaylor angles has median 1.0×10-3 and maximum 1.4×10-1. LAL evaluates all 200 draws.

Matches as pure functions

waveformx.match computes matches of frequency series on a common uniform grid with a given PSD, in pure JAX (jax.jit, jax.vmap, jax.grad). The overlap as a function of a time shift comes from a zero-padded inverse FFT; the best sample is refined by Newton steps on the exact overlap, so the result is smooth in the waveforms, and its gradient is that of the overlap at the best time. mismatch maximises over time and a constant phase. polarization_mismatch maximises the template F+h+ + F×h× over time and the antenna patterns, the norm of the projection of the signal onto the real span of the two polarizations (Harry et al. 2016); the phase is left to the binary, as it is not a free constant for precessing binaries with higher modes. with_conventions shifts a binary's reference phase and in-plane spin azimuths, and optimize minimises over them from several starts with L-BFGS-B and the gradient through the model. tests/test_match.py checks analytic cases (a flat band shifted in time gives the Dirichlet kernel, a constant phase gives cos φ, a signal with a part orthogonal to both polarizations gives its projection), the recovery of time and phase shifts off the FFT grid and of any combination of the polarizations, the derivatives against finite differences, and the recovery of shifted conventions of the lean IMRPhenomXPNR.

The comparison

The signal is NRSur7dq4's h+, through SimInspiralFD (LAL's, or our simulate_fd); the template is IMRPhenomXPNR at the same binary (LAL's SimInspiralChooseFDWaveformSequence, or ours), both with fref = 20 Hz and LAL's frame conventions, on 20–1024 Hz with Δf = 1/8 Hz and the aLIGO design PSD. The signal is the whole surrogate (fmin = 0), tapered by LAL at its start: conditioned from a frequency near the band, its tapered and filtered first cycles fall inside it, which changes the mismatch from 20 Hz by ~3×10−3 when conditioned from 20 Hz and ~10−4 from 10 Hz, as large as the differences being measured. Binaries are kept only where the surrogate covers the conditioning from 10 Hz. On the benchmark set, the whole surrogate and the surrogate conditioned from 10 Hz differ by at most 4.9×10-4; the whole surrogate starts lower, so what its own start contributes is smaller (from tests at 120–200 M☉, it falls quickly as the start moves below 10 Hz). Any such error is in the signal, common to LAL's model and ours.

The mismatch is maximised over time and polarisation, then optimised over what the two models define differently: the reference phase and a common rotation of the in-plane spins about the orbital angular momentum, the orientation of the frame in which the spins are given. Without the optimisation the mismatches are much larger (median 1.0×10-2 where LAL's models agree); nearly aligned binaries are best at a reference phase shifted by about −π/2, a difference of convention. The angle between the in-plane spins is physical and not optimised: freed, it lets the optimisation reach a different binary. The optimisation takes the best 3 of 16 starts (4 phases times 4 rotations) and runs Nelder–Mead from each, the same for LAL's model and ours: 189 evaluations per optimisation (median), at ~10 ms per model evaluation and ~4 ms per mismatch. The 40 binaries take 106.7 s on 16 processes, much of it compiling the models in each. From the best start alone, Nelder–Mead missed the optimum for a few binaries, by up to a factor of 6.

At the same mass-scaled reference. Both models take reference_mf, and they share the orientation conventions: the spins in a frame whose z axis is along the orbit (LN for IMRPhenomXPNR, the principal axis of the radiation for NRSur7dq4) and whose x axis points along the separation, the line of sight at the azimuth π/2 − phi_ref. They do not share the definition of the orbital phase at the reference. NRSur7dq4 sets the orbital phase to zero at the time its orbital frequency is the reference; IMRPhenomXPNR, like every IMRPhenomX model, sets the frequency-domain phase of the (2,2) mode at fref to 2phi_ref + π/4 with its time origin at the peak. In the stationary-phase picture that phase differs from twice the orbital phase by 2π fref t(fref), with t(fref) the time from the reference to the peak in each model, so the offset between the two descriptions is expected to depend on the binary, as it does. Over 36 aligned-spin binaries (q = 1.25, 2, 3; 100 and 160 M☉; three spin pairs; two inclinations) at reference_mf = 0.01, the mismatch of the same binary in both models is 8×10−4 to 0.17 without a shift of phi_ref, and 2×10−4 to 8×10−4 with the best one, which depends on the mass ratio and the spins (from 0.24 to 5.97 radians) but not on the total mass or the inclination (to 0.05 radians). A shared mass-scaled reference therefore removes the frequency convention but not the phase convention: comparisons still need the reference phase optimised, and for precessing binaries the in-plane spin rotation with it.

report/crossmodel.py --thorough checks the procedure, once (312.7 s). Nelder–Mead to tighter tolerances (259 evaluations) changes no mismatch by more than 1.4×10-3 of itself. The gradient optimisation of optimize through our model, from the same starts, agrees with Nelder–Mead to 3.4×10-8. Our NRSur7dq4 as the signal changes the mismatches by 4.9×10-12 of themselves.

Where our IMRPhenomXPNR sits

Our IMRPhenomXPNR departs from LAL 6.2.0's on this set by a median mismatch of 5.0×10-4 where LAL's models agree and 1.6×10-2 where they disagree. LAL used its SpinTaylor angles for 40 of the 40 binaries, so the departure is not a different prescription. It is almost all the parity fix (lalsuite !2593, which LALSuite's development branch has and 6.2.0 does not). The model before the break with LAL (tag lal-faithful; data report/data/crossmodel_lal_faithful.json) still had LAL's model and the fix as an option. Without the fix it agreed with LAL to 4.1×10-10, the fix alone changed it by up to 6.5×10-2, and its lean model differed from it, with the fix, by at most 8.6×10-6. Today's model departs from LAL by the same amounts as that lean model, to 5.0×10-6, so the breakdown still holds. The binaries where LAL's models disagree are those with large in-plane spins and mass ratios, where the fix matters most.

Where LAL's models disagree, our model is closer to NRSur7dq4 for 14 of 20 binaries, with a median ratio of its mismatch to LAL's of 0.89 (from 0.23 to 1.4). Where they agree, it is closer for 9 of 20, median ratio 1.02 (from 0.57 to 7.2): for a few binaries with in-plane spins around 0.4–0.6, the fix moves a waveform that matched NRSur7dq4 to 10−3 away from it by a few 10−3. So on balance the parity fix moves IMRPhenomXPNR towards numerical relativity where it is furthest from it. It is not uniformly an improvement. These are 40 binaries at 90–200 M☉, with NRSur7dq4's own error, not numerical relativity itself, as the reference.

Derivatives

For each model and four binaries, every column of the Jacobian (jax.jacfwd) is compared with central finite differences of the compiled model. The error of a column is max |Ji − Di| / max |Ji| over the frequencies or times and both polarizations. The finite difference is the better of a second-order difference with a step of 1e-6 and a fourth-order difference with a step of 1e-4, both relative for the masses, the distance and fref. The first is limited by rounding, the second by the model's discontinuities.

The derivatives are those of the function JAX computes, which is smooth almost everywhere but not everywhere. Where a model makes a discrete choice that depends on the binary, the waveform is continuous only up to the size of that choice's effect, and the derivative is that of the branch taken: the node where NRSur7dq4's integration starts, and in IMRPhenomXPNR the choices of the higher-mode amplitudes (the order of the intermediate polynomials, the vetoes of collocation points). IMRPhenomXPNR's precession angles are continuous: LAL's switches between prescriptions, the number of SpinTaylor samples and where LAL's adaptive integration stops do not enter the model. The spins are the parameters that move the precession most, and they show the largest disagreements; for nearly aligned in-plane spins the waveform is also far from linear on the scale of the step.

One case is not a jump but a point where no derivative exists. After every step of its dynamics, NRSur7dq4 rescales each spin to its initial magnitude (LAL's PrecessingNRSur_normalize_y). For a body with no spin, an infinitesimal spin then evolves by a factor that depends on its direction, not only on its size. The waveform therefore has a cone-shaped kink in that body's three spin components: one-sided finite differences tend to different values, and no step size makes them converge. At such a point the JAX derivative is finite, as the transform tests require, but it is a convention rather than a derivative. The table reports these components apart. As soon as a body has any spin, even an aligned one, its derivatives agree with finite differences as for the other binaries.

IMRPhenomXPNR. For the full IMRPhenomXPNR waveform, the finite differences agree with the derivatives to 1.9×10-7 at worst. At the tag lal-faithful they disagreed by up to ~10−2, most in the in-plane spins: LAL's finite differences in the intermediate region of the higher modes, with steps of 1e-9 and 1e-7, left rounding noise of ~1e-8 in the waveform that was not smooth in the parameters, and the SpinTaylor waveform jumped by LAL's integration error wherever the number of samples changed. The model takes exact derivatives and has no samples.

Memory

The arrays of a jitted function have fixed shapes, so IMRPhenomXPNR holds room for the longest case whatever the binary: the grid of the PNR interpolants has room for 524,288 points (a few thousand used for light binaries from 10 Hz; LAL's uniform grid for a binary neutron star from 10 Hz has about 290,000) and the SpinTaylor solution for 8,192 steps. A forward-mode derivative carries a copy of every array for each of the twelve parameters, and jax.vmap one for each binary, so that the batched Jacobian of 64 binaries needed 25.6 GB of temporary buffers (1.4 GB now). LAL's splines hold six arrays of it (α, β, γ and their second derivatives). Three changes, none of which changes the waveform beyond rounding (mismatch below 1e-15, Jacobians within 1e-13): the SpinTaylor steps hold 44 numbers instead of 58 and are read in place; under jax.vmap the binaries run in blocks of 16 (4 for the Jacobian), each block vectorised, about as fast as the whole batch at once; and above 16,384 frequencies the angles, modes and twist run over chunks of the frequencies (LAL's moving average of the antisymmetric amplitude, which reads all of them, first). One binary's waveform itself, without derivatives, takes chunks of 524,288 frequencies: XLA's kernels on a chunk of 16,384 are slower per frequency, which cost a long waveform ~20 ms. The interpolants of the phase grid (extended_phase_grid) also hold two arrays fewer (the splines of γ and of the unwrapped α rather than of γ ± α). The memory no longer grows with the batch, nor with the frequencies beyond the outputs themselves. It does not depend on the masses or fmin: the shapes are the same for every binary. The MLX port sizes its arrays to each binary's grid and was already lean: 64 binaries on 4,096 frequencies take – of GPU memory, – with the Jacobian.

Differences from LALSimulation

  • IMRPhenomXPNR: LAL's physics, not its numerics. The SpinTaylor angles for every binary, solved once with a high-order integrator and evaluated from its dense output; LAL's discrete switches of the angles made continuous; exact derivatives in the higher modes; no emulation of LAL's arithmetic; the parity fix of lalsuite !2593 (section 3). The version that reproduced LAL's numerics step for step, with LAL's fallbacks and the released model without the parity fix as options, is commit 0bbd3c5, tag lal-faithful.
  • Rounding, not bit-identity. waveformrs reproduces LAL bit for bit on macOS arm64, using the fused multiply-adds that clang emits. JAX has no portable fused multiply-add and XLA reorders arithmetic, so NRSur7dq4 and the layers of IMRPhenomXPNR below its precession angles agree with LAL to rounding. Only on the host, where the conversions' lengths and epochs hinge on the last bits, are LAL's fused multiply-adds reproduced exactly.
  • Solar masses, not kilograms. Every model, in both precisions, and the conversions' bounds on the duration compute with the masses in solar masses, times through MTSUN_SI and distances in Mpc, where LAL passes the masses through kilograms with G and c, which changes the results by rounding only.
  • The merger-ringdown continuation of the SpinTaylor angles. LAL attaches the merger-ringdown α and β to the SpinTaylor angles with their values and derivatives at 0.97–1.0 of the end of the inspiral, which it reads from its splines near their last knots: the natural end condition puts the derivatives 0.6–5% off the exact ones. The model takes them from the SpinTaylor solution, exact, which moves the merger-ringdown angles; for heavy binaries and late references the waveforms differ from LAL's by mismatches of up to ~2×10−4. This is an accepted difference: the model's values are the more accurate ones.
  • The zero crossings of the quintic intermediate amplitude. LAL's CrossZeroP5, which decides whether a higher mode's intermediate amplitude drops from a fifth-order polynomial, reads shifted entries of GSL's packed roots. It misses some roots and depends on the order in which GSL finds them, which rounding can change. Here the signs of the quintic at 257 points decide (hm/roots.py; at the tag, every root of GSL's companion matrix was tested with its own real and imaginary parts). Among the single-mode tests, one binary takes another polynomial for its (3,−3) mode, which then differs from LAL by a mismatch of 4.4e-7.
  • Values where LAL divides by zero. At isolated points of the parameter space, LAL's MSA formulas divide by an exact zero and produce inf or NaN. Here the values there are finite, and so are the gradients nearby, for example at equal masses or for a single spin.
  • No multibanding. LAL's SimInspiralChooseFDWaveform uses multibanding by default for IMRPhenomXPNR; waveformx, like waveformrs, evaluates every frequency. SimInspiralChooseFDWaveformSequence has multibanding off, and the conversions are compared with it off.
  • IMRPhenomD's quasi-normal-mode frequencies. They come from closed-form fits in the final spin rather than LAL's spline through its table: they differ by about a part in 106 and change the waveform by mismatches of 10−9 or less (section 6).
  • No data race. LAL's MSA and PNR angles race under OpenMP; its comparisons run single-threaded.
  • The scale of the output. Both models return the strain times 1021. scaled_strain=False, which WaveformOptions.lalsimulation_defaults() also sets, gives LAL's scale.

Limitations and future work

  • Six approximants. Only TaylorF2, IMRPhenomD, IMRPhenomXAS, IMRPhenomXHM, IMRPhenomXPNR and NRSur7dq4 are public; IMRPhenomXP and XPHM with their own flags are not exposed. TaylorF2, IMRPhenomD and IMRPhenomXAS have their polarizations at given frequencies only, not their modes or LAL's uniform grids; none has the conversions, nor in MLX LAL's errors (they give NaN). TaylorF2 has LAL's default orders only, without tidal terms. IMRPhenomD in MLX computes each binary's table in float32, so heavy binaries lose more to float32 there than in JAX. IMRPhenomXHM has LAL's defaults only (no mode arrays, release versions or multibanding).
  • Compilation time. IMRPhenomXPNR takes 15–20 s to compile, its gradient under a minute, and every array shape and batch size compiles separately. JAX's persistent compilation cache (jax_compilation_cache_dir) makes the cost one-off per machine. The tests use it; the package does not set it up.
  • Accelerators. The JAX versions ran on the CPU only. In JAX, both models run in float32 only through their pure functions, since the public functions and the conversions are float64; Neither model has been measured on a GPU through JAX. NRSur7dq4 and IMRPhenomXPNR run on Apple GPUs through their MLX versions (section 7, section 3), which have limits of their own:
    • it is float32 only, since MLX has no float64 on the GPU;
    • they cannot be differentiated in forward mode (mx.jvp) or mapped with mx.vmap, which MLX does not carry through custom Metal kernels. They take batches themselves; NRSur7dq4's gradients are reverse mode, IMRPhenomXPNR's are its forward-mode Jacobian contracted in a custom VJP;
    • IMRPhenomXPNR in MLX has only the pure function at given frequencies (no LAL grids or conversions), depends on JAX to trace the model, and sizes its arrays with the binaries (the SpinTaylor steps, the grid of the PNR interpolants), so it reads sizes back from the GPU and does not run under mx.compile.
  • Forward-mode derivatives of IMRPhenomXPNR. A gradient costs one Jacobian column per parameter with a non-zero tangent, evaluated together. That is affordable for the twelve parameters of a binary (section 12), but it does not scale to derivatives with respect to the frequencies or to many other inputs. A reverse-mode path would need the loops with traced lengths rewritten with fixed bounds.
  • Discontinuities. IMRPhenomXPNR inherits the discrete choices of LAL's higher modes (versions of the amplitudes by mass ratio and spin, vetoes, the order of the intermediate polynomials, the second intermediate region of extreme mass ratios, the collocation points of the (3,3) phase by the sign of the total spin); gradient-based samplers near such a boundary see the derivative of one side only. At zero in-plane spin the waveform is continuous but has no derivative, since LAL's fits depend on the magnitude of the in-plane spin. Making them smooth would change LAL's model.
  • Long waveforms. The conversions' Fourier transforms of light binaries from low frequencies hold millions of points, and the grid of the PNR interpolants keeps room for 524,288 nodes whatever the binary (a few thousand are used). These are fixed-shape arrays held in memory. Batches and frequencies are computed a part at a time (section 13), but the room of every binary computed together keeps its full size. extended_phase_grid differs from the default by mismatches up to ~2e-3 for binary neutron stars from 10 Hz (a near-constant offset of the angles above fref).
  • Multibanding. LAL's multibanding is not implemented (as in waveformrs).

Commit history

The commits of this branch, oldest first. The package was built model by model and milestone by milestone, each checked against LAL before the next. The conversions and the speed-ups of IMRPhenomXPNR were developed in parallel on two branches and merged. Commits that only add or regenerate this report are not listed. The repository has no remote.

Two bugs found while preparing an earlier version of this report, both fixed. First, the conversions and the IMRPhenomXPNR speed-ups had been developed on separate branches. After the merge, jax.grad of simulate_td_function failed with "Linearization failed to produce known values". The conversions called the model through a reverse-mode wrapper, while the speed-ups had made the model's loops forward-mode only. The conversions now use the model's forward-mode Jacobians, with respect to the binary and to the starting frequency, which itself depends on the binary (deeffce). Second, when the first use of NRSur7dq4's data was the pure function under jax.jit, the cached data were built inside the trace and leaked tracers into later calls. The tests had always read the data through a plain call first; the gradient measurements of section 12 did not (0a93b55, with a test).
