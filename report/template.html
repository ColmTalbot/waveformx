IMRPhenomXPNR and NRSur7dq4 in JAX, checked against LALSimulation

How two precessing waveform models, and LALSimulation's conversions between the time and frequency domains, were written as pure JAX functions that compile, vectorise and differentiate. The report covers how they compare with LALSimulation 6.2.0 and with waveformrs, how their derivatives compare with finite differences, and what they cost.

Summary

waveformjax implements the frequency-domain model IMRPhenomXPNR and the time-domain surrogate NRSur7dq4 in JAX, with the interface of waveformrs's Python bindings and LALSimulation's conventions. Every model is a pure function of a Binary pytree, so it can be compiled with jax.jit, evaluated for many binaries at once with jax.vmap, and differentiated with respect to all twelve parameters of the binary with jax.grad, jax.jvp and jax.jacfwd. LALSimulation's SimInspiralTD and SimInspiralFD are reproduced as well, with LAL's lengths and start times, and are differentiable too. The models follow LAL's code branch for branch, with each branch written as traced arithmetic. They agree with LALSimulation 6.2.0 to rounding for NRSur7dq4, and for IMRPhenomXPNR to the level at which different builds of LAL agree with each other.

In short:

  • Implementation. {{n_files}} Python modules ({{n_lines}} lines, of which {{n_lines_generated}} are parameter-space fits generated from waveformrs's Rust by a translator) cover the two models, their checks and the conversions (sections {{sec:design}}–{{sec:conversions}}).
  • Accuracy. NRSur7dq4 agrees with LAL at rounding level. IMRPhenomXPNR's (2,2) mode agrees to ~1e-9. Its higher modes agree to ~1e-5, because LAL takes derivatives by finite differences whose rounding noise JAX's different, but valid, arithmetic changes (section {{sec:accuracy}}).
  • Single precision. NRSur7dq4 runs in float32 when the binary's parameters are float32, and returns the strain times 1021 by default. In float32, an inner product between two different waveforms changes by {{f32_eps_median}} of the product of their norms (median; at most {{f32_eps_max}}). Derivatives agree with float64 to {{f32_grad_median}} (section {{sec:nrsur}}).
  • Derivatives. IMRPhenomXPNR is differentiated in forward mode, and its reverse-mode gradient is the transpose of that forward-mode Jacobian. NRSur7dq4 is differentiated directly in either mode. NRSur7dq4's derivatives agree with finite differences to ~1e-8. For IMRPhenomXPNR, finite differences of the full waveform disagree with the derivatives by up to {{xpnr_gradient_worst}}, most in the in-plane spins; the transform tests, which use the (2,2) mode, agree to ~1e-7. This is still being investigated (section {{sec:gradients}}).
  • Cost. A compiled IMRPhenomXPNR waveform costs about as much as LALSimulation's, and a few times as much as waveformrs's. jax.vmap over many binaries is how JAX code would use it. Compilation takes tens of seconds for the model and minutes for its gradient (section {{sec:performance}}).

Two bugs found while preparing this report, both fixed. First, the conversions and the IMRPhenomXPNR speed-ups had been developed on separate branches. After the merge, jax.grad of simulate_td_function failed with "Linearization failed to produce known values". The conversions called the model through a reverse-mode wrapper, while the speed-ups had made the model's loops forward-mode only. The conversions now use the model's forward-mode Jacobians, with respect to the binary and to the starting frequency, which itself depends on the binary (deeffce). Second, when the first use of NRSur7dq4's data was the pure function under jax.jit, the cached data were built inside the trace and leaked tracers into later calls. The tests had always read the data through a plain call first; the gradient measurements of section {{sec:gradients}} did not (0a93b55, with a test).

Models as pure JAX functions

The package has the interface of waveformrs's Python bindings, with the same function names, keyword arguments, options, result classes and exceptions. Underneath, every model is a pure function of a Binary: a NamedTuple of float64 arrays (or float32 arrays, for NRSur7dq4), and so a JAX pytree.

import jax, jax.numpy as jnp, numpy as np
import waveformjax as wf

frequencies = np.arange(20.0, 1024.0, 0.125)
hp, hc = wf.polarizations("IMRPhenomXPNR", frequencies, mass_1=36.0, mass_2=29.0,
                          spin_1=(0.4, 0.2, 0.3), spin_2=(-0.2, 0.3, -0.1), f_ref=20.0)

# the pure function, for jit, vmap and grad
model = wf.polarizations_function("IMRPhenomXPNR")          # f(binary, frequencies)
binary = wf.Binary(mass_1=36.0, mass_2=29.0, spin_1=jnp.array([0.4, 0.2, 0.3]),
                   spin_2=jnp.array([-0.2, 0.3, -0.1]), f_ref=20.0)
loss = lambda b: jnp.sum(jnp.abs(model(b, frequencies)[0]) ** 2)
jax.grad(loss)(binary)                                      # a Binary of derivatives
jax.vmap(model, in_axes=(0, None))(binaries, frequencies)    # many binaries at once

# NRSur7dq4 gives the strain times 1e21 (scaled_strain=False: the strain itself)
h = wf.td_polarizations("NRSur7dq4", 1 / 4096, 20.0, mass_1=40.0, mass_2=20.0)
surrogate = wf.td_polarizations_function("NRSur7dq4")       # f(binary, times)
single = wf.Binary(mass_1=jnp.float32(40.0), mass_2=jnp.float32(20.0), spin_1=jnp.zeros(3, jnp.float32),
                   spin_2=jnp.zeros(3, jnp.float32), f_ref=20.0)
hp, hc = surrogate(single, h.times)                         # in float32
h = wf.simulate_td("IMRPhenomXPNR", 1 / 4096, 20.0, mass_1=36.0, mass_2=29.0)

A call goes through four stages:

  1. Checks on the hostThe public functions convert the arguments and run LALSimulation's checks on concrete values (MODEL.validate), outside any JAX transformation. Invalid masses, spins or frequencies, or a precession version LAL would replace while version_fallback=False, raise the exceptions of waveformrs: InvalidParameterError, PrecessionVersionUnavailableError and so on.
  2. A compiled function per configurationThe model method is compiled with jax.jit with the options fixed. WaveformOptions is a frozen dataclass, so it can be a static argument. JAX caches the compiled function per options and array shape, so a sampler that keeps its frequency grid compiles once.
  3. Traced arithmeticInside, nothing branches in Python on the binary. Each of LAL's ifs becomes a jnp.where between branches that are both evaluated, with safe arguments, so that the branch not taken cannot put NaN into the value or the gradient. Loops of fixed length are lax.scan; loops whose length depends on the binary are lax.while_loop. The precession prescriptions are branches of a switch.
  4. Failures as NaNFailures that LAL can only detect while computing, such as a SpinTaylor integration that cannot reach the reference frequency, make the output NaN. The batch functions report those binaries as ModelError in errors and set their rows to zero.

Importing the package enables 64-bit floats (jax_enable_x64), which IMRPhenomXPNR needs; NRSur7dq4 also runs in float32 (section {{sec:nrsur}}). The thousands of fitted coefficients of IMRPhenomX are not copied by hand. scripts/rust_to_python.py parses the straight-line Rust of waveformrs (its let bindings, arithmetic, fma calls and f64 methods) and emits the same expressions on JAX arrays. scripts/generate_fits.py then writes them into models/xpnr/_generated/. The control flow is written by hand, file by file after LAL's, and each module's docstring names the LAL function it follows.

Each model package defines a MODEL with a fixed contract. A frequency-domain model has validate, polarizations, modes and choose_fd_modes. A time-domain model has validate, td_polarizations, td_modes and polarizations_at, the last of which evaluates the waveform at any given times. The public functions, the batches and the conversions are written once, against that contract.

IMRPhenomXPNR

IMRPhenomXPNR is IMRPhenomXPHM with the phenomenological PNR precession angles, a co-precessing (2,2) mode tuned to numerical relativity, an antisymmetric (2,|2|) contribution and the 122019 release of the higher modes. In LALSimulation it draws on many of the 49 IMRPhenomX source files, together with the SpinTaylor code and GSL. Here it is about 14,800 lines of Python, of which 4,400 are generated fits.

Four prescriptions in one compiled function

LALSimulation chooses the Euler angles by the binary. In-plane spins below 1e-7 give plain IMRPhenomXPHM with the NNLO angles, without the PNR machinery. Below 1e-6, the PNR machinery runs with the NNLO angles (version 102). Otherwise the SpinTaylor angles are used (version 330). Where LAL's SpinTaylor integration fails, it falls back to the MSA angles (223), or to NNLO if the MSA system cannot be set up. Here the decision is a traced index, and the prescriptions are three branches of a switch; versions 102 and 223 of the PNR machinery share one branch, chosen by a traced flag. Under jax.vmap, lax.switch with a batched index would compute every branch for every binary. The model's own switch, cond and while_loop (_transforms.py) instead have batching rules: a branch is computed for the whole batch only if some binary in the batch takes it, and a loop runs to the longest binary in the batch while selecting only its small carry at each iteration.

The SpinTaylor angles

The SpinTaylor angles are the hardest part to make traceable. LAL integrates the orbit-averaged SpinTaylorT4 equations from f_ref backwards to below f_min and forwards to the end of the inspiral. It uses GSL's adaptive RKF45 at tolerances of 1e-12, samples the solution every Δt = 5 / fcut, repeats the end with ten times finer sampling, and passes all the samples, millions for light binaries, to natural cubic splines of α and cos β. Where each run ends, and so how many samples there are, depends on the exact sequence of steps. The JAX version therefore splits the work:

  • The forward and fine runs repeat LAL's adaptive steps one for one in a while_loop (spintaylor/replica.py): GSL's step control, the retries after a failed derivative, the Hermite-like interpolant and the stopping test. The run then ends where LAL's ends, with the same samples. Its derivatives are those of the recorded steps with the step sizes held fixed.
  • The backward run is long and only ends at a frequency, so it is solved with Dormand–Prince 8(5,3) and its seventh-order dense output. The steps are equal increments of u = 1 / (Mω), so each step covers about the same precession angle, and a few hundred steps resolve a whole inspiral to ~1e-12. The step size is a smooth function of the state, so the solution is differentiable. It differs from LAL's samples by LAL's own integration error, ~1e-9.
  • The splines have as many knots as LAL has samples. Up to 8192 knots they are built as GSL builds them. Beyond that, a natural cubic spline at x depends on knots k intervals away only through factors ~(2 − √3)k. It is then evaluated from the 34 knots around x, which gives the same values to ~1e-15 at a cost per point independent of the number of samples.

Reproducing LAL's discrete choices

Much of LAL's code makes decisions on values that are themselves results of iterations or of NaN arithmetic. Each has to be reproduced exactly, or the waveform changes discontinuously:

  • MSA angles. GSL's Jacobi elliptic function and elliptic integral are ported with a fixed number of iterations, and the iterations GSL would not do are masked out. Unlike LAL, which stores the roots of the spin cubic in a shared struct and races on them under OpenMP, every value here is local, so the angles agree with single-threaded LAL.
  • Higher modes. The order of the intermediate amplitude polynomial is lowered when it would cross zero. CrossZeroP5 takes the roots from GSL's gsl_poly_complex_solve, and LAL's indexing of the roots depends on their order, so GSL's balanced companion matrix and Francis QR iteration are ported step for step in while_loops that end where GSL's end.
  • PNR β connection. LAL picks the connection frequencies of β from the turning and inflection points of a cubic. The computation often produces NaN (complex roots, square roots of negative numbers), which LAL's comparisons then treat as false. It is evaluated twice. The first pass uses LAL's arithmetic, NaN included and under stop_gradient, to make the decisions. The second uses finite stand-ins that follow those decisions, to give the values and their gradients. The inflection points are roots from the trigonometric formula, close to an acos of 1, so there LAL's fused multiply-adds are reproduced exactly, giving ~1e-12 rather than ~1e-8.
  • Antisymmetric amplitude. LAL smooths the amplitude ratio with an 81-point moving average computed in place, so each window reads averages it has already written. That is an order-40 linear recurrence. It is evaluated in blocks of 40 values with a triangular solve per block, inside a lax.scan.

Derivatives in forward mode

Loops whose number of iterations is traced, such as the SpinTaylor runs and GSL's QR iteration, can be differentiated in forward mode but not in reverse mode. Reverse mode through the model's many scalar set-up computations also compiles slowly. So _transforms.differentiable makes the model a jax.custom_jvp function. Its rule computes the Jacobian with respect to the parameters that have non-zero tangents, one jax.jvp per parameter, and contracts it with the tangents. The contraction is linear, so jax.grad, which transposes it, works as well as jax.jvp and jax.jacfwd. The Jacobian is itself a custom_vmap function, so under jax.vmap of a gradient the binaries are batched inside the differentiation. Where LAL takes derivatives by finite differences, with steps of 1e-9 and 1e-7 in the intermediate region of the higher modes, the values follow LAL. Their tangents, however, are those of the exact derivatives. The tangents of the finite differences themselves would amplify rounding by up to 1e14.

The parity fix

By default the model includes the fix of lalsuite !2593 from LALSuite's development branch, as waveformrs does. The fix restores the mirror symmetry of the antisymmetric modes: flipping the in-plane spins and taking ι → π − ι gives (h+, −h×). pnr_parity_fix=False, or WaveformOptions.lalsimulation_defaults(), gives the released LALSimulation 6.2.0. Both are compared with LAL in section {{sec:accuracy}}, the fixed model with a LALSimulation 6.2.0 patched with the fix ({{xpnr_patched_build}}).

Making it compile and run faster

The first complete version took about 32 ms per waveform and 2.3 s for jax.vmap over 64 binaries; compiling its reverse-mode gradient took 714 s. A second round of work brought these to about 18 ms, 0.74 s and 132 s (measured during development on this machine). It moved the derivatives to forward mode and added the batch-aware branches and loops. It merged versions 102 and 223 into one branch, computed only the PNR β of the binary's region, chose the intermediate polynomials of all higher modes together, and solved the SpinTaylor equations with the 3-vectors as arrays. The current numbers are in section {{sec:performance}}.

NRSur7dq4

NRSur7dq4 interpolates numerical-relativity waveforms of precessing binaries. It was trained on mass ratios up to 4 and spin magnitudes up to 0.8, and LAL allows it to extrapolate beyond those. It integrates an ODE for the coprecessing frame, the orbital phase and the spins, with time derivatives given by fits at 233 nodes. It then evaluates the coorbital modes from fits at their empirical-interpolation nodes and rotates them to the inertial frame. The JAX version follows LALSimIMRPrecessingNRSur.c and waveformrs's translation of it.

  • Data. The 17 MB HDF5 file is read once with h5py. Every dynamics fit becomes a dense row over the union of the monomials the fits use, so the nine fits of a node are one matrix–vector product. The fits of the waveform nodes are stored by the order of their mass-ratio monomial, so the mass ratio, which is the same at every node, is folded into the coefficients once per binary.
  • Dynamics. LAL steps from the reference frequency to the nearest node, takes three RK4 steps (forwards or backwards, depending on that node), and covers the remaining nodes with fourth-order Adams–Bashforth steps in both directions. Since the starting node depends on the binary, the integration is one lax.scan of fixed length. It walks backwards from the start and then forwards, evaluating the fits of one node per step as LAL does. The Adams–Bashforth weights depend only on the node and are tabulated once.
  • Splines. GSL's natural cubic splines solve a tridiagonal system whose matrix depends only on the knots. As in waveformrs, it is factorised once when the data are read, so fitting a spline needs only the two substitution sweeps.
  • Frame rotation. LAL's Wigner D-matrices use negative powers of the quaternion's components, with special cases where they vanish, as they do for spins along the orbital angular momentum. Here the negative powers are cancelled analytically, which makes each element a polynomial in the quaternion: the same values, no special cases, and finite gradients in the non-precessing limit.
  • Output grid. The number of samples depends on when the orbital frequency reaches π fmin, so it is found on the host. A compiled pass evaluates the frequency fits, and numpy repeats LAL's search operation by operation. The waveform is then computed on a power-of-two buffer of at least 256 samples, which waveforms of similar length share, and trimmed to the binary's length.

polarizations_at(binary, times) evaluates the waveform at any times relative to the peak. It is pure and differentiable with respect to every parameter, including f_ref, which moves the reference time and so the frame. It is what jax.vmap, jax.grad and the conversions use.

Single precision and the scale of the output

NRSur7dq4 is evaluated in the precision of the binary's parameters. A Binary of float32 arrays is evaluated in float32 throughout, with a float32 copy of the data cast from the float64 arrays (the spline factorisations included). A test checks that no operation of the traced waveform, or of its gradient, is in float64. The public functions take Python numbers, so they stay in float64. Two changes make float32 usable:

  • The output is the strain times 1021. This applies to every NRSur7dq4 function, the public functions and the conversions included, unless scaled_strain=False. The strain itself fits in float32, but its square (~10−42) and a detector's power spectral density (~10−47) do not. The comparisons with LAL in this report use scaled_strain=False.
  • Nothing passes through SI units. LAL converts the masses to kilograms and back (m * MSUN_SI / MSUN_SI) and works with the distance in metres. In float32, XLA folded the square of the solar mass in kilograms (~4×1060, beyond float32's range) into the derivative of the mass ratio, so the compiled gradient was NaN. The derivative with respect to a distance of ~1025 m underflowed to zero. The masses now stay in solar masses, and the amplitude is one constant times the total mass over the distance in Mpc. In float64 this changes the waveform by rounding only.

What float32 costs in accuracy was measured on {{f32_pairs}} pairs of binaries, drawn as for the accuracy comparison (report/float32_nrsur7dq4.py). {{f32_pairs_each}} pairs are two independent binaries, whose overlaps are near zero. The other {{f32_pairs_each}} are a binary and a small perturbation of its masses, spins and angles, with a median overlap of {{f32_nearby_overlap}}. Both binaries of a pair are evaluated on one time grid, in float64 and in float32, projected on a detector and tapered at the start. Inner products use the aLIGO design sensitivity from 20 Hz, without maximisation, and are computed in float64 unless stated otherwise.

The dynamics, a sequence of 230 small steps, take {{f32_dyn_share}} of a float64 batch. Keeping them in float64 would make the inner products about ten times more accurate, at the cost of part of the speed-up on the CPU; the package does not do this. float32 matters most on accelerators, where float64 is much slower, and there it has not been measured.

Time and frequency domains

simulate_td and simulate_fd reproduce LALSimulation 6.2.0's SimInspiralTD and SimInspiralFD. IMRPhenomXPNR can be given in the time domain and NRSur7dq4 in the frequency domain, and each model can also be conditioned in its own domain. They follow waveformrs's src/conversions/ and src/conditioning.rs.

LAL's conditioning starts the model below the requested frequency by an amount set by bounds on the chirp time, merger time and ringdown time. It tapers the extra length, applies a Butterworth high-pass filter, shifts the waveform in time and crops it to a length and epoch derived from the same bounds. The work is split by what needs to be exact:

  • On the host, in Python floats. The lengths, shifts, taper widths and GPS epochs are computed with LAL's arithmetic. This includes C's pow, cbrt and round, and the fused multiply-adds of clang's arm64 build, reproduced exactly through rational arithmetic. As a result, lengths and epochs are LAL's bit for bit.
  • In JAX, pure and differentiable. The bounds are computed again where the waveform depends on them continuously: the starting frequency given to the model and the cutoff of the first high-pass filter. So do the filter coefficients, the filter itself (a lax.scan of LAL's recursion per second-order section), the tapers and the Fourier transforms. Arrays have fixed, power-of-two lengths, so one compilation serves many binaries.

IMRPhenomXPNR smooths its antisymmetric amplitude with a moving average over the array of frequencies, which LAL takes over its band. To keep one array length per transform length, the model is evaluated on the band, followed by frequencies just above it and then the band's last 82 frequencies again. The first L − 41 values are then averaged as LAL averages them, and the last 41 are taken from the end. simulate_td_function and simulate_fd_function return the conversion as a pure function. It keeps the lengths, shifts, tapers and epoch of a reference binary, while the starting frequencies and filter cutoffs follow the binary, so the gradient includes their dependence on the masses and spins.

How the package is tested

{{n_tests}} pytest tests ({{n_passed}} passed, {{n_failed}} failed, {{n_skipped}} skipped in the run below) compare every layer of the models with LALSimulation 6.2.0, through its Python API or, for internal functions, through ctypes. They also reuse waveformrs's reference suites, which cover each regime switch and fallback of the models.

The tests are layered as the model was built: the (2,2) mode, then the higher modes, the angles, the PNR helpers, the polarizations and the modes. Each layer is compared at a tolerance set by what LAL itself can resolve there. The MSA angles, for example, are ill-conditioned for nearly aligned spins, where LAL's own output changes by ~5e-8 for a change of the inputs in the last bit. Every model also has a transform test. It checks that jax.jit and jax.vmap give the plain call, that gradients agree with central finite differences for every parameter, and that they are finite at removable singularities such as aligned, vanishing or single spins.

Accuracy

The random draws are those of waveformrs's report, so the two reports can be compared. For IMRPhenomXPNR: total masses log-uniform in 5–300 M☉, mass ratios uniform in 1/20–1, isotropic spins of magnitude up to 0.99, fmin ∈ {10, 15, 20} Hz, fref = fmin or up to 3 fmin, and isotropic lines of sight. For NRSur7dq4: mass ratios 1–6 with either body first, total masses 20–300 M☉, sampling at 1–8 kHz, and fmin above the surrogate's start or zero.

NRSur7dq4 agrees with LAL to {{nrsur_max_rel}} at most (median {{nrsur_median_rel}}), with LAL's length and epoch in every compared draw ({{nrsur_same_grid}}/{{nrsur_n}}). {{nrsur_failure_text}} The residual comes from summing the fits in a different order and from XLA's own exp, sin and cos. The rotation to the inertial frame and the splines are linear, so the errors stay at rounding level. waveformrs, which agrees with LAL bit for bit, gives the same comparison ({{nrsur_rs_max_rel}}).

IMRPhenomXPNR agrees with LAL 6.2.0 to {{xpnr_max_rel}} at most, with median {{xpnr_median_rel}}, a 90th percentile of {{xpnr_p90_rel}} and mismatches below {{xpnr_max_mm}}. The tests show where the difference comes from. The (2,2) mode alone, with its PNR angles and antisymmetric part, agrees to ~1e-9. The higher modes agree to ~1e-5, as different builds of LAL agree with each other. LAL fixes the intermediate amplitude of each higher mode with a fourth-order finite difference of the inspiral amplitude, with a step of 1e-9, which turns rounding of 1e-16 into ~1e-7 in the derivative. It fixes the intermediate phase of the (3,2) mode with a second derivative of a phase of up to 1e4 rad, by central differences with a step of 1e-7, amplifying rounding by ~1e14. Without fused multiply-adds, which JAX does not expose portably, the JAX arithmetic rounds differently from LAL's arm64 build, and these finite differences turn that into a visible difference. Neither value is more accurate than the other. The errors grow with the total mass, where the merger and ringdown, and so the higher modes, carry more of the band: the median is {{xpnr_median_light}} below 20 M☉ and {{xpnr_median_heavy}} above 150 M☉. The largest errors, a few 1e-5, are for heavy binaries with in-plane spins near 0.9. {{xpnr_frac_1e5}} of the draws exceed 1e-5.

With the parity fix, the model agrees with LAL 6.2.0 patched with lalsuite !2593 to {{xpnr_fix_max_rel}} (median {{xpnr_fix_median_rel}}, mismatch ≤ {{xpnr_fix_max_mm}}, over {{xpnr_fix_n}} draws). On the same draws waveformrs agrees with that patched library to {{xpnr_rs_fix_max_rel}}. {{xpnr_failure_text}}

Derivatives

For each model and four binaries, every column of the Jacobian (jax.jacfwd) is compared with central finite differences of the compiled model. The error of a column is max |Ji − Di| / max |Ji| over the frequencies or times and both polarizations. The finite difference is the better of a second-order difference with a step of 1e-6 and a fourth-order difference with a step of 1e-4, both relative for the masses, the distance and fref. The first is limited by rounding, the second by the model's discontinuities.

{{gradients_figure}} {{gradient_table}}

The derivatives are those of the function JAX computes, which is smooth almost everywhere but not everywhere. Wherever LAL makes a discrete choice that depends on the binary, the waveform is continuous only up to the size of that choice's effect, and the derivative is that of the branch taken. Such choices include the number of SpinTaylor samples, where LAL's adaptive integration stops, the node where NRSur7dq4's integration starts, the precession prescription, and the order of the intermediate polynomials of the higher modes. In IMRPhenomXPNR the SpinTaylor waveform jumps by about LAL's integration error, ~1e-9 relative, wherever the number of samples changes. A finite difference that straddles such a jump is wrong by the jump divided by the step, which is why small steps are used. The spins are the parameters that move the precession most, and they show the largest disagreements. For nearly aligned in-plane spins the waveform is also far from linear on the scale of the step. None of this affects the derivatives themselves, which are those of the smooth pieces.

One case is not a jump but a point where no derivative exists. After every step of its dynamics, NRSur7dq4 rescales each spin to its initial magnitude (LAL's PrecessingNRSur_normalize_y). For a body with no spin, an infinitesimal spin then evolves by a factor that depends on its direction, not only on its size. The waveform therefore has a cone-shaped kink in that body's three spin components: one-sided finite differences tend to different values, and no step size makes them converge. At such a point the JAX derivative is finite, as the transform tests require, but it is a convention rather than a derivative. The table reports these components apart. As soon as a body has any spin, even an aligned one, its derivatives agree with finite differences as for the other binaries.

IMRPhenomXPNR: under investigation. For the full IMRPhenomXPNR waveform, the finite differences disagree with the derivatives by up to {{xpnr_gradient_worst}}, by far the most in the in-plane spins. The transform tests, which compare the gradient of a weighted sum of the (2,2) mode with finite differences, agree to ~1e-7. The likely cause is in the higher modes. Their intermediate region is fixed by LAL's finite differences with steps of 1e-9 and 1e-7, which leave rounding noise of ~1e-8 in the waveform. That noise is not smooth in the parameters, so a parameter step of 1e-6 sees a slope of up to ~1e-2 relative from it alone, while the JAX tangents are those of the exact derivatives. If so, the derivatives are right and the finite differences are not. The check still to run: the (2,2) mode alone, and the full model with larger steps, where that noise matters less.

Differences from LALSimulation

  • Rounding, not bit-identity. waveformrs reproduces LAL bit for bit on macOS arm64, using the fused multiply-adds that clang emits. JAX has no portable fused multiply-add and XLA reorders arithmetic, so waveformjax agrees with LAL to rounding (NRSur7dq4, the (2,2) mode of IMRPhenomXPNR) or to the rounding amplified by LAL's own finite differences (the higher modes, ~1e-5). Only where LAL's decisions hinge on the last bits are its fused multiply-adds reproduced exactly: the PNR inflection points and, on the host, the conversions' lengths and epochs.
  • SpinTaylor backward integration. The integration from f_ref down to f_min is a dense Dormand–Prince solution, not LAL's RKF45 steps. It differs from LAL's samples by LAL's integration error (~1e-9).
  • Values where LAL divides by zero. At isolated points of the parameter space, LAL's MSA formulas divide by an exact zero and produce inf or NaN. Here the values there are finite, and so are the gradients nearby, for example at equal masses or for a single spin.
  • No multibanding. LAL's SimInspiralChooseFDWaveform uses multibanding by default for IMRPhenomXPNR; waveformjax, like waveformrs, evaluates every frequency. SimInspiralChooseFDWaveformSequence has multibanding off, and the conversions are compared with it off.
  • No data race. LAL's MSA and PNR angles race under OpenMP. waveformjax agrees with single-threaded LAL.
  • The scale of NRSur7dq4's output. NRSur7dq4 returns the strain times 1021, and computes the masses and the amplitude without SI intermediates, which changes its float64 values by rounding. scaled_strain=False, which WaveformOptions.lalsimulation_defaults() also sets, gives LAL's scale.
  • Two options that are not LAL's defaults. With version_fallback=False the compiled model contains only the SpinTaylor prescription and is NaN where LAL would switch to the MSA or NNLO angles. With extended_spintaylor=True it is a different model: the SpinTaylor angles for every binary, with LAL's discrete switches made continuous. A separate report, report/extended, maps where the SpinTaylor angles fail and describes and validates the extended model.

Limitations and future work

  • Two approximants. Only IMRPhenomXPNR and NRSur7dq4 are public. IMRPhenomXAS and the 122019 higher modes exist as parts of IMRPhenomXPNR, but IMRPhenomXHM, XP and XPHM with their own flags, TaylorF2 and IMRPhenomD are not exposed.
  • Compilation time. IMRPhenomXPNR takes tens of seconds to compile, its gradient minutes, and every array shape and batch size compiles separately. JAX's persistent compilation cache (jax_compilation_cache_dir) would make the cost one-off per machine; it is not set up by the package.
  • Accelerators untested. Everything here ran on the CPU. IMRPhenomXPNR uses float64 JAX operations, which are slow on consumer GPUs. NRSur7dq4 also runs in float32, but only through its pure functions, since the public functions and the conversions are float64. Neither model's accuracy or speed has been measured on a GPU.
  • Forward-mode derivatives of IMRPhenomXPNR. A gradient costs one Jacobian column per parameter with a non-zero tangent, evaluated together. That is affordable for the twelve parameters of a binary (section {{sec:gradients}}), but it does not scale to derivatives with respect to the frequencies or to many other inputs. A reverse-mode path would need the loops with traced lengths rewritten with fixed bounds.
  • Discontinuities. The waveform inherits LAL's discrete choices. Gradient-based samplers near a regime boundary, such as in-plane spins near 1e-6, or a SpinTaylor integration that just fails, see the derivative of one side only.
  • Long waveforms. For light binaries from low frequencies the SpinTaylor splines have millions of knots and the conversions' Fourier transforms millions of samples. These are fixed-shape arrays held in memory, and both cost and compilation grow with them.
  • Multibanding. LAL's multibanding is not implemented (as in waveformrs).
