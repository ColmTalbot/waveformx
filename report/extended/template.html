IMRPhenomXPNR without fallbacks: where the SpinTaylor angles fail, how to extend them, and a continuous model

LALSimulation's IMRPhenomXPNR switches between three prescriptions for the precession angles, and between many branches inside them. This report maps where its main prescription, the SpinTaylor angles, fails. It then extends that prescription to every binary and replaces LAL's discrete switches with continuous versions, so that the waveform and its derivatives are continuous in the binary's parameters. Where LAL keeps the SpinTaylor angles, the extended model agrees with LAL to a mismatch of {{val_max_low}} or better for mass ratios up to 12.

Summary

IMRPhenomXPNR uses the SpinTaylor precession angles (version 330) for {{frac_ok}} of {{n_draws}} random binaries. For the other {{frac_fail}}, LAL falls back to the MSA angles. Every one of these failures has the same cause: the reference frequency, where the spins are given, lies beyond the frequency at which the post-Newtonian (PN) binding energy stops decreasing, so LAL's integration of the SpinTaylor equations backwards from it stops at once. The extended model integrates through that region. It evaluates every binary, and its waveform and derivatives are continuous across all the switches LAL makes between prescriptions and branches.

Three variants of the model

LAL decides the precession prescription from the binary. In-plane spins below 10−7 switch the PNR machinery off; below 10−6, the NNLO angles are used; otherwise the SpinTaylor angles (version 330), unless their integration fails, in which case LAL uses the MSA angles (223), or the NNLO angles if the MSA system fails too.

Where the SpinTaylor angles fail

For {{n_draws}} random binaries, waveformrs (which reproduces LAL to rounding) was asked for IMRPhenomXPNR with version fallbacks switched off. The draws: total masses log-uniform in 5–500 M☉, mass ratios uniform in 1/20–1, isotropic spins of magnitude up to 0.99, fmin ∈ {10, 15, 20} Hz, and fref = fmin for 30% of them, otherwise uniform up to min(5 fmin, fmax). {{n_fail}} of them ({{frac_fail}}) fail.

One cause. All {{n_fail}} failures end LAL's backward integration from fref with the energy code ({{fail_codes}}). LAL integrates the SpinTaylor equations backwards from fref to below fmin and forwards to the end of the inspiral. After every step it applies the SpinTaylor stopping test, and the first test is whether the PN binding energy still decreases with frequency. If fref already lies beyond the frequency where it stops decreasing (a PN analogue of the minimum energy circular orbit, which depends on the masses and spins), the backward run stops after its first step with a single sample, and LAL's driver falls back to the MSA angles. Evaluating the same test at fref separates the two groups exactly: it is at most {{max_fail_test}} for every failure and at least {{min_ok_test}} for every binary tested that keeps the angles.

The failures need a reference frequency late in the inspiral: M fref ≥ {{min_mfref_fail}} in every case. That happens for heavy binaries (the lightest failure is {{min_mass_fail}} M☉, the median {{median_mass_fail}} M☉), for reference frequencies above fmin, and earlier for spins anti-aligned with the orbit, which move the PN energy minimum to lower frequencies.

Extending the SpinTaylor angles

The SpinTaylor equations themselves remain regular beyond the energy test: the derivative of the orbital frequency stays positive from fref down to fmin in every failure examined, so they can be integrated backwards from fref. Only the stopping test, which guards the validity of the forward evolution, ends LAL's run.

  1. Integrate backwards through the energy testWhere the test fails at fref, the backward run ignores it and continues to below fmin, so the spins given at fref are carried to lower frequencies by the same equations. Where LAL's backward run succeeds, the test never fails along it (the energy test only fails at high frequencies), so nothing changes there.
  2. End the inspiral where the PN energy test holdsThe knots of the inspiral angles stop at the last sample of the backward run that passes the energy test, found by bisection. From there the fine run goes on to where the energy test fails, as LAL's does for a reference frequency just below it. The PN angles are therefore never used beyond the validity of the PN equations, and the merger-ringdown continuation takes over as usual.
  3. A larger step budgetwaveformjax integrates the backward run with DOP853 on a fixed number of steps in u = 1/(Mω), estimating the time of each step from the leading-order evolution. From frequencies beyond the energy test that estimate is poor in the first steps, so the budget is 25% above the range rather than 3%.
  4. Drop checks on an unused quantityFor a few very heavy binaries the whole band lies after the end of the inspiral, and LAL's checks on its own interpolation of γ (IMRPhenomX_InterpolateGamma_SpinTaylor) fail. The PNR angles never use that γ (they integrate their own on the PNR grid), so the extended model skips those checks.
  5. Every in-plane spinThe thresholds at 10−7 and 10−6 are removed: the PNR machinery and the SpinTaylor angles are used down to aligned spins. Where the in-plane spin vanishes, the J frame equals the L frame, and the angles are evaluated without division by zero (see section {{sec:continuous}}).

With these changes, the extended model evaluates all {{ext_finite}} of the {{n_fail}} binaries that LAL cannot evaluate with the SpinTaylor angles. There is no reference waveform for them, but two checks show the extension behaves as an extension should. First, it is continuous with LAL's SpinTaylor waveform across the reference frequency at which LAL gives up: for a 300 M☉ binary, LAL's waveform jumps by a mismatch of {{fref_jump_lal}} at fref = {{fref_boundary}} Hz (where it changes to the MSA angles), while the extended one changes by {{fref_jump_ext}} (relative) across the same interval, 10−9 (relative) on either side. (Within ~10−12 of that frequency LAL's decision is not monotonic: the energy test there is at the level of rounding.) Second, its mismatch with LAL's MSA fallback is of the size expected between two different angle prescriptions: median {{msa_median}}, 90th percentile {{msa_p90}}.

The discontinuities of LAL's model

Besides the choice of prescription, LAL's waveform depends on many discrete quantities: how many samples each SpinTaylor run has, the size of the grid of the PNR interpolants, and the branches of the PNR fits. Each is a jump in the waveform as a function of the parameters. A gradient sees none of them, but samplers and finite differences do.

Method. Five binaries were scanned along four parameters each (m1, χ1x, χ1z, fref) at 101 points, recording every discrete quantity of the set-up. Wherever one changes between neighbouring points, bisection locates the change to adjacent floating-point values, and the jump of the waveform across it is measured as |h+(x⁺) − h+(x⁻)| / |h+| with aLIGO design weights. LAL's switches that depend on fixed thresholds were measured directly across the threshold.

The edges of the PNR calibration region (q = 8.5, χ = 0.85) are continuous in LAL, by design. Three switches are not:

  • The edge of the transition region, q = 12. Inside the region, the PN part of β is the opening angle of the optimal emission direction (Eq. 41 of arXiv:2107.08876) behind an arctangent window; outside, it is the dynamical opening angle. The higher modes also change their frequency map there. These differ at the edge, by a mismatch of up to a few 10−3.
  • The two-spin construction, |χ2| = 10−3. Above this threshold, LAL tapers the SpinTaylor β into the β of an equivalent single-spin MSA system near the connection frequency and uses that system in the connection. These are two different PN approximations of the same angle, so the jump does not shrink as χ2 → 0. Over {{ts_n}} random binaries the jump has median {{ts_median}}, 90th percentile {{ts_p90}} and maximum {{ts_max}}: {{ts_frac}} exceed 10−3. It is largest for 4 ≤ q < 12 (median {{ts_mid_median}}) and below {{ts_low_max}} for q < 4.
  • The connection frequencies of the PNR β. PNR joins the inspiral β to its merger-ringdown fit at a lower connection frequency, and holds β at the minimum of the fit beyond an upper one. Both are chosen by a decision tree on the shape of the fit: which root of a cubic is its inflection point, whether the fit turns over, rules that switch formula at M f = 0.06, and whether the fit is attached at all. Along {{beta_n}} decision changes found by bisection on random lines through spin space, {{beta_visible}} change the waveform visibly, {{beta_big}} by more than 10−3, the largest by {{beta_lal_max}}.

The thresholds in in-plane spin (10−7, 10−6 and 10−3, the last for the remapping of the J frame) give smaller jumps, as does the end of LAL's SpinTaylor angles in fref, which is a switch to the MSA angles.

Numerical noise. A change of m1 in its last bit changes LAL's waveform by up to {{noise_rs_xpnr}} (waveformrs; {{noise_rs_xhm}} for IMRPhenomXHM, {{noise_rs_xas}} for the (2,2) mode alone). Almost all of it is in the {{noise_worst_mode}} mode (up to {{noise_worst_mode_value}} relative). LAL fixes the intermediate phase of that mode with the second derivative of the mode-mixed ringdown phase by central differences with a step of 10−7, which amplifies rounding by ~1014. waveformjax's default model already differentiates this exactly for its gradients, but its values keep LAL's finite differences.

A continuous model

The extended model replaces each discontinuity with a continuous version, chosen to stay as close to LAL as the discontinuity allows: where LAL jumps between two values, any continuous version must lie away from at least one of them near the jump.

The end of the inspiral

LAL's fine run ends at the end of the integration step in which the stopping test first fails, and its samples sit on a lattice counted from the start of the run. The extended model finds the time tstop at which the stopping condition is met on LAL's interpolant of the last step: the PN energy or v = 1 crossing zero, the frequency reaching its bound, or the second derivative of the frequency vanishing. It uses bisection, then a Newton step, so that the derivatives are those of the root. Its last knot lies half a lattice step before tstop, at the centre of the range in which LAL's last sample falls, and the fine knots are counted back from there. The end of the angles, the spins at the end (used by the PNR fits) and the knots near the end (where the merger-ringdown angles are fitted) then all move continuously with the parameters. A knot still appears or leaves where the fine run meets the coarse knots, about a hundred knots earlier, which changes the splines only at the level of their interpolation error. Centring matters: for binaries whose frequency rises quickly near the end, a continuous end placed at tstop itself differed from LAL by a systematic half step.

Small and vanishing spins

Without the in-plane thresholds, the model has to stay regular as the in-plane spin vanishes. The precession angle α = atan2(ly, lx) of L in the J frame is set to zero with zero derivative where both components vanish. The effective single-spin cosine of the PNR fits is kept 10−15 away from ±1, because the generated fits take √(1 − cos²θ) and arccos(cos θ) with infinite derivatives there; this changes their sine by less than 5 × 10−8. The spin magnitude and the cosine of the PN waveform β are computed without dividing zero by zero for non-spinning binaries. The remapping of the J frame is applied for every in-plane spin, which removes LAL's switch at 10−3. As the in-plane spin goes to zero, the extended model tends to within a mismatch of ~2 × 10−7 of LAL's waveform with the PNR machinery switched off.

The two-spin construction for every binary

The extended model uses LAL's two-spin construction for every binary, including those with χ2 = 0 exactly and non-spinning ones, so there is no switch at |χ2| = 10−3; its jump there is {{ts_ext_max}} at most over the same {{ts_n}} binaries. For |χ2| ≥ 10−3 (all but a fraction ~10−3 of a uniform spin prior) this is LAL's model. For smaller χ2 it differs from LAL by up to the size of LAL's jump, mostly for q between 4 and 12. Two other choices were considered: LAL's single-spin construction everywhere (which would change almost every binary), and a ramp between the two over a range of χ2 (which only spreads the change). The two-spin construction is finite, with finite gradients, for χ2 = 0, χ1 = 0 and both zero. Its cost is a finer PNR grid where |χ2| < 10−3, the same grid LAL uses for any larger χ2.

The connection of the PNR β

The decision tree of IMRPhenomX_PNR_BetaConnectionFrequencies and IMRPhenomX_PNR_AttachMRBeta needed three kinds of change.

  • Accurate inflection points. Most of the large jumps found along random lines were not LAL's design but rounding. The inflection points of the fit are the roots of a cubic whose leading coefficient passes through zero within the parameter space. LAL's trigonometric formula then loses most of the digits of the two finite roots, about 10−2 of them for b/a ~ 107. It collapses them onto the vertex of the quadratic, and LAL's choice of root jumps between them. The extended model computes the real roots robustly: it brackets each between the stationary points of the cubic (from the stable form of the quadratic formula), bisects, and takes one Newton step. It keeps LAL's order of the roots and LAL's choice among them. The chosen root is then continuous through the change of sign of the leading coefficient.
  • Blends instead of choices. Each choice between two candidate frequencies is blended across a band of ±0.002 in M f of the quantity it compares: the rules at M f = 0.06, whether the fit turns over, which way Eq. 69 picks the root, and whether the maximum lies above the lower candidate. The blend uses a C² ramp, and only where both candidates are finite; elsewhere LAL's choice stands. Holding β at the minimum of the fit becomes a weight that goes from one to zero as the minimum falls below the inflection point, instead of the upper frequency jumping to 100. Attaching the merger-ringdown β becomes a weight blending the attached and detached β across the same tests: in M f for the lowest connection frequency, in radians for the upper bound on β, and in the sign of β relative to its size for the lower bound. Where the coefficient of the cubic changes sign, the maximum of the fit runs off to infinity and comes back from the other side. The fit's shape does not change in any waveform's band, but LAL's turnover test does, so beyond |M f| = 10 the two outcomes are blended in 1/(M f) of the maximum, which passes continuously through zero.
  • Bands that scale. Some decision variables shrink with the spin. For small or nearly aligned effective spins, the step from the maximum of the fit (Eqs. 56–57) is ~10−5 in M f, inside any fixed band, without LAL's choice changing. A first version that blended it differed from LAL by a mismatch of 0.4 for one such binary. That choice is kept as in LAL, and the attachment test on the sign of β is normalised by the size of β.

A blend replaces a jump with a steep but continuous change, so the waveform is continuous but can change quickly within the bands. At the steepest point found, the mismatch between points a step apart falls from {{steps_first}} at {{steps_first_h}} to {{steps_last}} at {{steps_last_h}}, as the square of the step: the signature of a continuous function.

Beyond the transition region

For mass ratios above 12 the agreement required was relaxed to a mismatch of 10−1. LAL's model is kept exactly for q ≤ 12 and for q ≥ 14. Between them, β is blended from LAL's value at the edge of the transition region to LAL's value outside, and the frequency map of the higher modes from LAL's linear map to its inspiral scaling, with a C² weight in q. Along a line in q at 120 M☉, the extended model differs from LAL by at most {{q12_below}} below q = 12, {{q12_band}} between 12 and 14, and {{q12_above}} above 14.

Exact derivatives in the higher modes

In the extended model, the higher modes use the exact derivatives (from jax.jvp along the frequency) for their values as well as their gradients, where LAL takes finite differences: the derivative of the inspiral and ringdown amplitudes, and the first and second derivatives of the mode-mixed (3,2) ringdown phase. The change of the waveform for a change of the mass in its last bit drops from {{noise_lal}} to {{noise_ext}}, so values and gradients are now consistent, and finite differences of the waveform can be taken with small steps.

Agreement with LAL

Over {{val_n}} random binaries (same distribution as above) for which LAL keeps the SpinTaylor angles, the extended model agrees with LAL to a mismatch of {{val_median}} in median and {{val_p99}} at the 99th percentile. For mass ratios up to 12, the largest mismatch is {{val_max_low}}, within the required 10−3; {{val_frac_1e4}} of these binaries exceed 10−4. Of the {{val_n_high}} binaries above q = 12, {{val_high_over}} exceeds the 10−1 allowed there, with {{q12_worst_mismatch}}: for that binary, LAL's own waveform jumps by a mismatch of {{q12_worst_jump}} at q = 12. A model that is LAL's up to q = 12 and continuous has to differ from LAL by about that jump just above 12, so no continuous choice meets 10−1 there. The mismatch uses aLIGO design weights, 1024 uniformly spaced frequencies from fmin to min(0.3/M, 1024 Hz), no maximisation, and the larger of the two polarizations.

Near LAL's own jumps the extended model necessarily differs from LAL: a binary that sits on one side of LAL's switch is compared with a continuous model that already moves towards the other side. Derivatives

The extended model is differentiated as the default one is, with forward-mode Jacobians behind jax.grad. Central differences approach the gradients as the step shrinks, until the numerical noise of the waveform divided by the step takes over. For the extended model they agree with the gradient to ~2 × 10−7 in the mass and to ~10−4 in an in-plane spin component over steps from 3 × 10−3 to 3 × 10−5, before the noise (~10−9) takes over below ~10−5. The in-plane plateau does not shrink with the step as a truncation error would, so it is a residual at that level that we have not traced. For LAL's model, with noise ~10−6 from its finite differences in the higher modes and the jumps of section {{sec:switches}}, the differences in the in-plane spin do not settle at any step.

With steps of 3 × 10−5 (relative for the masses, the distance and fref), in the range where the differences resolve the derivative, they agree with the gradients to {{gradient_worst}} or better for every parameter of three binaries, all modes included.

What remains

  • Choices kept from LAL. The step from the maximum of the PNR β fit (Eqs. 56–57) is still chosen as LAL chooses it, so a jump remains where the slope of the fit at its inflection point changes sign. So do LAL's fail-safes (no connection when the lower frequency is negative or NaN, a single real inflection root, a vanishing cubic). These are rare in random draws, but they were not targeted by the jump survey.
  • Interpolation-level jumps. The grid of the PNR interpolants and the junction of the coarse and fine SpinTaylor knots still change size with the parameters, which moves the waveform by the interpolation error of the splines (the PNR grid and first-sample rows of the jump table).
  • Steep bands. A blended switch is continuous but steep. The bands are narrow in the quantities LAL compares, which can be narrow in the binary's parameters. A sampler crossing one sees a large gradient rather than a jump.
  • No NR check. Where the extended model departs from LAL — beyond the energy test, for |χ2| < 10−3, inside the blends — its choices are made for continuity and closeness to LAL, not checked against numerical relativity. The region where the two-spin choice matters most, 4 ≤ q ≤ 12, lies largely outside NRSur7dq4's range.
  • A residual in the in-plane derivatives. Finite differences in an in-plane spin component agree with the gradient to ~10−4 but no better at any step (section {{sec:derivatives}}). Something at that level, perhaps one of the interpolation-level steps above, remains to be found.
  • Gradients at aligned spins. They are finite, but they are not the in-plane derivatives of the waveform (section {{sec:derivatives}}).
